# Burst, bend and the correct scouting observable

Research push, October 14, 2026. Actual competition tracking was used throughout. The push corrected receiver break alignment, separated receiver speed ceiling from timed-dash performance, audited defensive-front drill and NFL populations, constructed nonlinear source profiles, and examined persistent player pressure-rate error rather than only noisy individual rush error. The useful result is a specific exploratory defensive-front lead, with substantial sensitivity and uncertainty retained. It is not a guaranteed scouting advantage or a claim that more model complexity guarantees a prize.

## Data and position-specific questions

The defensive-front cohort contains 74 participating rookies: 23 from 2023, 22 from 2024 and 29 from 2025. There are 4,769/4,814/5,082 eligible player-play records, or 14,665 total. The source population provides 177 valid tracked forty attempts and 568 skill-drill attempts in pass rush, run the hoop, four-bag agility, and start/wave/lateral reaction. The tracked source feature family contains 26 sprint-response descriptors and 14 descriptors for each of four skill drills, 82 coordinates before missingness handling and compression.

Use defensive ends, defensive tackles, nose tackles and outside linebackers. Require rookie regular-season, non-nullified records with finite charted get-off, a source profile, a unique ball-snap frame and at least 30 records per player after exclusions. The target pressure indicator is specifically whether the supplied time-to-pressure timestamp is observed among those get-off-charted records. This is not a survival analysis in which every missing timestamp has a known censoring time. All charted sacks in this cohort have a pressure timestamp, but this does not independently establish universal completeness of the pressure-event charting.

The first question is whether sensor motion predicts charted get-off beyond familiar measurements and starting alignment. The second is whether it improves prediction of a player's recorded pressure tendency across a workload. Get-off is not treated as reaction time alone. NFL starting depth and lateral position are derived from actual tracking at ball snap, with the line of scrimmage reconstructed from field position, possession and play direction. No later NFL movement or pressure outcome is used as a covariate.

## Source movement features

Sprint fits use the retained first- and second-order response families. For normalized elapsed z=max((t−delay)/tau,0), speed is V(1−exp(−z)) or V(1−exp(−z)(1+z)). Fit capacities, response times, delays, fit error and Jacobian conditioning; add fixed-time speeds/accelerations, Legendre shape coefficients and an early/late speed ratio. These are fitted finite phenomenological responses, not proofs of exact athlete dynamics.

The initial skill measurement begins with three consecutive speeds above 0.5 yd/s, then uses up to five seconds of tracking. It computes speed at 0.5/1.0/1.5 seconds, early acceleration, high speed, integrated braking, speed-weighted angular travel, geometric bending energy, straightness, speed–acceleration signed area, jerk, and times to accumulate one/three/five yards of path length. Path length is not forward displacement, and the onset threshold is not an observed official cue. Smooth positions with a seven-frame cubic Savitzky–Golay filter; position-derived angular rates and speed-derived acceleration retain sensor and filtering limitations. Body-weight interactions use standardized weight, not literal force or energy in physical units.

For a path z=(speed,acceleration), the signed channel area is one half of the sum of midpoint(speed) times acceleration increment minus midpoint(acceleration) times speed increment. Order matters even when marginal summaries agree. Positive-only angular measures remove left/right sign as an arbitrary drill-orientation distinction. Repeat attempts are aggregated by player/drill median. All imputation, scaling, source regression on conventional physical attributes, and principal-component compression use only training players. Four or six latent components receive squared and body-mass interaction terms. Those components are observations of covariance, not identified biological mechanisms.

## Baselines and validation

The conventional baseline contains provided body, timed and NGS measures; position and lined-up role; snap depth, width and speed; down, distance, field position, score, quarter, offensive formation, blitz indicator and teams. All four model families also receive the same five source-drill availability indicators. This prevents mere collection or attendance information being counted only as movement innovation. The initial prototype without those flags is retained separately and is not the strong-baseline result.

Each prospect receives equal total training/evaluation weight, regardless of number of snaps. Four class-stratified outer player folds and three inner player folds select among three ridge settings and two fixed forest settings. All observations of a player stay together. A separate 2023 training/2024 validation split selects forward configurations; refit on earlier classes and evaluate 2025. Model families are conventional, sprint, skill and combined. No 2025 score chooses a configuration. The wider research program previously inspected WR/DB 2025 outcomes, and the present sequence is adaptive; every result is described as exploratory.

## Why a scouting gain can be hidden by rush-level error

Let e_it=y_it−p_it for player i and rush t. With equal total weight per player, the exact finite decomposition is

\[
L_{\mathrm{rush}}=\frac1P\sum_i\left[\bar e_i^2+\frac1{n_i}\sum_t(e_{it}-\bar e_i)^2\right],
\qquad L_{\mathrm{player}}=\frac1P\sum_i\bar e_i^2.
\]

The player-level quantity measures systematic calibration of a pressure-rate estimate over that player's actual recorded workload. It is a different question from calling one rush correctly. Source information can reduce persistent athlete error while barely changing noisy event-level prediction. It does not imply an 11% increase in pressures, and it is conditional on NFL workloads and context rather than an unconditional pre-draft production forecast.

The player-rate metric was added after inspecting play-level results, so it is explicitly secondary. The combined source model's exploratory 2025 player-rate MSE was 0.000623689 versus 0.000704235 for the conventional baseline, an 11.44% point reduction across 29 players. Rate RMSE fell from about 2.65 to 2.50 percentage points. The absolute MSE improvement was 0.000080547, with conditional player bootstrap interval [0.000020953, 0.000145124]. Team resampling over 21 teams yielded [0.000024907, 0.000146948]. Leaving any one forward player out retained a positive mean improvement, ranging from 0.000063516 to 0.000089340.

The corresponding rush-level Brier/MSE improvement was only 0.24%, from 0.045378653 to 0.045268539, with an interval crossing zero. The two magnitudes are compatible through the decomposition above. Get-off did not show a transferable gain: combined source MSE was 0.057374154 versus baseline 0.056678110 in 2025. These are different outcomes and both are retained.

## Strengthen the comparison rather than selecting a convenient reference

Select the conventional and source banks separately using player-rate error on earlier-player inner folds and 2024. The forward conventional and combined choices remained the same forest configuration, retaining the 11.44% forward point change. But the stronger player-metric-selected earlier baseline improved: MSE 0.001254713 versus combined 0.001211940, only a 3.41% earlier-player point change, with absolute-improvement interval [−0.000160351, 0.000279842]. This qualification matters; the original 13.0% earlier-player figure is not the fairest task-specific baseline comparison.

Two further original-protocol development partitions yielded positive combined player-rate differences. Across the same 45 earlier players, the three differences averaged 0.000209041, conditional interval [−0.000005507, 0.000460232]. These reuse the same players and are not three independent studies. Thirty whole held-out source profiles were swapped within NFL position using the fixed earlier-trained combined model. Real player-rate MSE beat 28 of 30 swaps. Permutation may break physical/profile dependence or source availability, and there is no exchangeability-certified significance claim or reselected-model permutation p-value.

Eighty training-player bootstrap refits resampled separately within 2023/2024. Replicated copies received distinct temporary training IDs so prospect weights preserve bootstrap multiplicity; retaining the original ID while normalizing each player's total weight would cancel repeated draws. Refits recomputed all transformations and models, holding earlier selected configurations fixed. Their fixed-forward-player difference interval was [−0.000588447, 0.000155337]; joint training/forward-player resampling gave [−0.000609155, 0.000180640]. These much wider intervals expose training instability that the prediction-conditional intervals omit. Full model selection and adaptive metric-search uncertainty remain outside even these refits. The favorable point estimate is therefore a lead, not an established population-wide advantage.

## Adapt source alignment using the actual traces

A source-only audit compared the initial movement portion with faster parts of the event. Across four-bag events, the median initial five-second mean speed was approximately 1.55 yd/s, versus approximately 4.10 yd/s for the fastest five-second portion; lateral-reaction values were approximately 2.69 versus 4.35. A low threshold can include preparatory movement. This does not prove every early phase is setup, but it invalidates silently treating the initial low-speed segment as the official athletic drill start.

The follow-up onset requires three consecutive speeds above 2 yd/s, preserving the NFL cohort and giving every baseline the revised availability flags. It is an operational athletic-onset hypothesis, not labeled ground truth. The earlier-player combined rate MSE improved to 0.001209457 from 0.001254713, but forward MSE was 0.000733216 versus 0.000704235. The 11.44% initial-phase point gain did not survive this onset revision. Reporting only the favorable threshold would misrepresent the evidence. Stable, cue-anchored phase definitions are a concrete requirement for a future validated scouting metric.

Whole-athlete bagging is the final variance adaptation in this push: average equally budgeted conventional and combined models trained on class-stratified prospect resamples, with earlier-selected configurations fixed. Separate outer-player ensembles and forward ensembles preserve the original player boundaries before replica IDs are assigned. Both the initial-phase and athletic-onset versions are retained. Their figures are reported in the accompanying aggregate receipts and the completed result addendum; no unfavorable ensemble is dropped.

## Receiver follow-ups

The angular-progress receiver study replaces a small first heading adjustment with a sustained qualifying break. Fixed route angle ranges cover IN/OUT/SLANT; stable before/after headings, approach speed and flanking motion gates reject walking turns. The response is aligned between 20% and 80% of heading rotation and follows through exit speed, within observation before pass arrival. This is an algorithmic full-break candidate rather than hand-labeled playbook truth. There were 337 source attempts from 106 receivers and 2,937 eligible NFL turns from 57 receivers across classes. Source execution did not improve exit-speed ratio versus source-demand baselines. Earlier-player MSE was 0.044137754 versus 0.043226915; forward 0.040414171 versus 0.040227751.

The pure pre-draft receiver speed-ceiling study had 43 earlier and 16 forward receivers, with at least 30 measured routes each. Target is the player 90th percentile of route 95th-percentile speeds, measured from snap+1 second through pass release. Inputs include no NFL team, state, role mixture or route-count predictor. The baseline also receives conventional late-speed proxy 30/(forty−ten-yard-split) and initial-acceleration proxy 20/(ten-yard-split squared), avoiding a weak linear-timing reference. These are algebraic proxies, not exact sprint dynamics.

The tracked-ceiling model reduced initial earlier-player MSE from 0.150763165 to 0.140011328, a 7.13% point change; its interval crossed zero. Three earlier partitions averaged absolute improvement 0.012719559, interval [−0.005642640, 0.034139480]. Forward improvement was only 0.72%, 0.086684773 to 0.086059219, interval [−0.003381316, 0.004632147]. Adding full speed-shape features worsened forward performance. NFL route-speed capacity is opportunity-dependent and is not separation, production, injury risk or proof of hidden talent.

## Reproduction and practical use

The self-contained defensive-front notebook embeds the source modules, checks all nine official acquisition hashes, reconstructs source profiles and snap alignment, fits strong baselines, computes both error metrics, repeats development splits, swaps profiles, performs eighty refits and selects a player-metric baseline. It writes private checkpoints outside public output directories; checkpoint resumption preserves completed work after interrupted long jobs. The source CSVs, credentials and individual predictions remain excluded from Git and the archive. Standalone scripts reproduce receiver, onset and athlete-bagging follow-ups. Aggregate receipts preserve model banks, populations, uncertainty and failures.

The professionally defensible finding is that the scouting observable should be a persistent player trait rather than only individual-play accuracy, and that measurement phase and training-population uncertainty materially affect an apparent gain. A team could use this framework to test a role-conditioned movement metric prospectively. The present evidence does not yet justify an athlete development intervention, a draft-bust avoidance claim, or guaranteed contest victory. It provides a quantitative candidate, the comparisons needed to judge it, and explicit tests the candidate has not passed. No public Kaggle publication or formal submission has occurred.


## Completed athlete-averaging result

Sixteen whole-athlete models per family were averaged within each earlier outer fold and in forward fitting, with matched budgets for conventional and combined predictors. On the earlier player holdout, conventional MSE was 0.000997646 and combined 0.000888341, a 10.96% tracking point increment, conditional absolute-improvement interval [−0.000000660, 0.000235993]. Forward conventional MSE was 0.000575076 and combined 0.000547147, a 4.86% tracking point increment, interval [−0.000012286, 0.000074129]. This is a stronger predictor and a smaller, uncertain incremental tracking claim.

Compared with the earlier unbagged task-specific conventional model, athlete averaging alone reduced earlier MSE by 20.49% and forward MSE by 18.34%. Combined averaging/tracking reduced forward error by 22.31% from 0.000704235 to 0.000547147. Its conditional improvement interval against that earlier baseline was [0.000006491, 0.000350109]. This overall algorithmic comparison must not be relabeled as a 22% sensor-only advantage: most comes from giving the conventional predictor the same correct athlete-level averaging. Adaptive model choice and full training uncertainty remain material.

The faster-onset ensemble reduced earlier MSE to 0.000910243 versus conventional 0.000997646, an 8.76% point change. In 2025 it instead scored 0.000593958 versus 0.000575076, 3.28% worse, interval on improvement [−0.000058099, 0.000019909]. Thus initial-phase tracking remains phase-sensitive even after variance adaptation. The durable candidate engineering improvement is to match resampling to the athlete generalization unit; the sensor-specific scouting interpretation still needs better cue/phase identification and independent confirmation.

The averaging rationale also has a finite identity. For predictions p_b and their mean p_bar, mean_b[(y−p_b)^2]−(y−p_bar)^2 = mean_b[(p_b−p_bar)^2] ≥ 0. No independence assumption is needed for this algebra. It compares the average component error with the ensemble error, not the ensemble with an arbitrary unbagged reference; the latter improvement is established only for the recorded empirical benchmark.
