## Measure the two phases directly

All nine official CSVs were reacquired through the enrolled account and checked against retained hashes. The unchanged front cohort comprises 74 rookies and 14,665 eligible charted-get-off records. Tracking, rather than assumptions about the supplied get-off annotation, defines the new target.

Let the operational onset be the first of three contiguous frames at or above a speed gate following the unique ball snap. Reject already-active-at-snap records, gaps, incomplete windows, lack of sustained onset within two seconds, and failure to cover one geometric yard after onset. The primary speed gate is 1 yard/second; 0.5 and 2 are sensitivity definitions. With smoothed positions $p_k$, let

$$L_j=\sum_{k=1}^{j}\|p_k-p_{k-1}\|,\quad \tau_{on}=t_{start}-t_{snap}.$$

Interpolate the first crossing of $L=1$ to obtain $\tau_{travel}$, then $\tau_{total}=\tau_{on}+\tau_{travel}$. This is smoothed geometric arc travel. It is not necessarily backfield progress, verified time to contact, an official line-crossing measure or neurological reaction time. Faster arc travel can reflect a lateral assignment and is not automatically superior football performance.

The primary definition retains all 74 players and 14,057 records, with median onset about 0.30 seconds and median next-yard travel about 0.467 seconds. The 0.5 gate retains 11,723 records; the 2 gate retains 13,573, each still covering all players. Cohort selection remains conditional on the earlier charted-get-off eligibility rule. ## Fold-local prediction and a concrete cache repair

Primary prediction target is each player's average NFL post-onset travel residual relative to a fixed context reference. The context reference uses observed snap geometry, situation, NFL role, formation and teams solely to construct the response. It is rebuilt in each inner and outer split. Training residuals use three whole-player crossfits inside training players; evaluated residuals use a reference trained only on that split's training players. No evaluated labels construct training targets, and no NFL tracking/context enters pre-draft predictors.

A cache issue was found during the multi-gate experiment: the old context key used target name and player-ID sets, so changed records or labels could reuse a stale model. Content hashing now includes training record identities, contexts and target labels, plus evaluated identities/contexts excluding evaluated outcome labels. Tests demonstrate that changing training labels produces a new reference, while perturbing evaluated labels preserves cache identity and training fitting. Old aggregate results are retained; this patch is not a claim that every earlier historical result was affected.

Conventional predictors include 15 body/timed/NGS attributes, Combine position and shared source counts. Compare raw burst and repeat-precision profiles. For each source channel, train a trait-based reference and estimate within-repeat variance and residual between-player variance. A player's source correction is multiplied by $\rho_i=\tau^2/(\tau^2+\sigma^2/n_i)$. Variance estimates and trait maps use training players only. This is empirical shrinkage under sparse repeats, not a calibrated posterior.

Primary raw-burst earlier reductions are −8.16%, −1.26% and −0.28%; repeat-precision gives −0.13%, −2.36% and −0.06%. Positive means less squared error. Forward changes are −0.72% raw and −1.66% repeat-precision. There is no consistent incremental advantage beyond conventional information.

The 0.5-gate supporting result loses 15.17% raw and 13.00% precision forward. The 2-gate precision model improves earlier error 1.75%, with a positive fixed-prediction paired interval, but loses 2.24% forward. That is not stable transfer. Supporting onset precision improves forward error 3.46%, while losing 6.19% earlier; raw onset loses 7.69% forward. These definitions and families are all retained, without promoting the favorable forward figure to a primary success.

An adaptive onset control, declared before its own scores, compares the trait-predicted source reference alone and the reliability-scaled individual deviation alone. Reference-only changes are −1.47% earlier and −0.07% forward; individual-increment changes are −2.28% earlier and conventional fallback forward. The positive precision-onset score is not simply reproduced by the trait-reference control, but these tests still do not establish a stable individual movement effect. Adding a transformed trait basis can alter regularization, so raw model-family differences are not automatically isolated new-information gains.

A posthoc top-quarter audit reports every family/fold. On the primary 29-player forward comparison, all systems identify three of the eight observed fastest residual targets. The audit is descriptive, was added after MSE inspection, and does not establish a frozen or selection-adjusted decision advantage. ## The useful new finding: a more repeatable mechanical response

On the same primary records, 200 random game-halves give median player rank correlations 0.428 for onset, 0.855 for post-onset travel, 0.636 for combined time and 0.787 for supplied get-off. Travel's 2.5–97.5% split-sensitivity range is 0.802–0.900. These ranges are not ordinary confidence intervals, a latent-talent measure or a physical prediction ceiling. The response is materially more stable across observed game halves, which narrows future modeling to a clearer mechanical channel. Source burst profiles have not yet provided a validated incremental forecast of it.

The next bounded question is whether direction-specific useful progress or matched situations preserve this repeatability while making the response more football-relevant. Exact observability and finite planning can identify missing measurements; they cannot manufacture predictive information or guarantee competition victory.
