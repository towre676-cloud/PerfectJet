# Where a cut ends and recovery begins

The new push tests a substantially more specific hypothesis than global sprint-to-speed or drill-to-separation prediction: does a receiver's execution of a turn, after accounting for its approach speed and angular demand, predict how that receiver handles corresponding movement demands in the NFL? It also tests receiver separation and yards after catch. The experiments have not established a substantial predictive gain. They have produced a more complete measurement pipeline, a promising but unconfirmed YAC candidate, and a concrete diagnosis of the information that current source measurements leave unresolved. All unfavorable comparisons are retained.

The original design and program hashes were recorded before this push extracted its new NFL responses. That is a record of a fixed experimental procedure, not a claim of a globally preregistered or fresh blind test: the broader program had already inspected 2025 WR results. The original algorithm chose the first qualifying local heading-change peak, with a six-frame angular window, at least one second between detected peaks, 25–150 degree angle, minimum approach speed 2.5 yards/second, and adequate flanking motion. Game anchors occur between 0.5 and 3 seconds after snap, before pass release. Mapped routes are IN, OUT, SLANT and HITCH; the source mapping uses Dagger, Speed Out, Slant, Curl and Comeback. These operational anchors can capture an initial heading adjustment rather than a full playbook route break. All support and transfer conclusions are conditional on that definition. Different turn definitions would require separately disclosed experiments rather than silently rewriting this result.

Source extraction produces 495 complete attempts from all 106 supplied WRs. Sixty-six candidate attempts have no qualifying first turn and three lack the full response window. The NFL candidate population has 7,306 rookie regular-season non-nullified mapped route records. Unique snap/release and sampling checks reject 888 missing/nonunique event records and six short or irregular sequences; 3,773 have no qualifying pre-release turn. There are 2,630 anchored turns. These exclusions define a narrow observation population: in particular, the study does not recover route breaks centered after an anticipatory pass release.

Let c be the turn anchor and v be mean supplied speed over c−0.5 to c−0.3 seconds. The primary response is B=∫₀⁰·⁵ max(0,1−s(c+h)/v) dh, evaluated on six observed 10 Hz speed samples. B has units of seconds and lies in [0,0.5] for nonnegative speed. Angular demand is the absolute heading change across c±0.3 seconds, measured from smoothed positions; equal-speed redirection impulse is 2v sin(angle/2). Other source coordinates measure local minimum-speed retention, mean exit speed normalized by entry speed, and canonical curve area normalized by squared arc length. These are descriptive movement quantities. Smaller B is not universally better football performance: stopping routes intentionally dissipate speed, and catch involvement or tactical termination can change the post-turn trace.

The original response measurement stopped at pass release. This left 1,234 of the 2,630 anchors without a full subsequent half-second. After a ten-window/player threshold, the primary population comprised 21/16/14 receivers and 599/430/287 turns across 2023/2024/2025. Its 2025 execution model worsened normalized-deficit MSE from 0.00116237 for the source-demand comparator to 0.00122875. The incremental difference was −0.00006638 with interval [−0.00014258,0.00000894]. Earlier nested comparison was slightly favorable but uncertain, and separation comparisons were unfavorable. Those outputs are preserved rather than replaced by the revised analysis.

A development-only timeline audit establishes that all 1,211 qualifying 2023 player-routes have at least 1.3 seconds of supplied tracking after release. Median follow-up after release is 3.2 seconds. Consequently truncation at release was an analysis-imposed loss of response information, not the end of the supplied sensor record. A second recorded design keeps exactly the same pre-release anchors and extends only response observation. All 2,630 anchored half-second windows are now measured, an 88.40% increase over the 1,396 originally complete windows. This is a genuine gain in measurement coverage; it is not a gain in NFL prediction accuracy.

The full-follow-up cohort has 23/18/15 players and 1,174/851/517 turns, totaling 56 players and 2,542 turns after the unchanged ten-window threshold. No anchor is moved using later outcomes. Ninety-percent speed recovery is followed through 1.5 seconds or the actual trace end, whichever comes first. Recovery requires two consecutive qualifying samples after the local speed minimum. Distribution-free recovery bounds retain early unresolved records without an independent-censoring assumption. At half a second, the original lower and upper recovered-fraction bounds were 0.68023 and 0.91065. With full follow-up they coincide at 0.71293. The longer 1.5-second endpoint still has 544 right-censored records, including athletes who have not recovered within that horizon. Bounds here describe observed records and are not player-balanced estimates of a universal recovery probability.

Within each model-training set, source outcomes are regressed on approach speed, angle, their product and their squares using fixed ridge penalty ten. Source-response residuals are aggregated by player and mapped route, then residualized against standardized conventional physical attributes. Four execution coordinates supply linear, squared, game-demand and mass interaction terms. Every transformation uses training players only. The conventional baseline includes timed Combine tests, body measurements, NGS scores and supplied football context plus measured NFL turn demands. The source-demand control additionally includes matched Combine approach speed, angle, impulse and accepted-attempt count, including availability information. Thus the primary execution contrast must improve beyond source demand and missingness, rather than exploiting the fact that a player happened to have a qualifying recording.

Every prospect receives equal total fitting and evaluation weight. Four class-stratified outer player folds contain every route of their held-out players. Three inner player folds tune the same five candidate configurations for each family: ridge penalties one, ten and one hundred, a depth-six random forest, and depth-eight extra trees, with 150 trees and minimum leaf size twenty. A distinct 2023 training/2024 tuning comparison selects a setting before refitting earlier classes and evaluating 2025. Pooled earlier folds assess unseen players; the forward comparison changes both prospect class and rookie season. Prior test inspection still prevents describing 2025 as a new confirmatory trial. Shared team, quarterback and tactical effects are not fully resolved by player resampling.

After correct follow-up, earlier nested normalized-deficit MSE is 0.00275992 for source demands and 0.00279164 for execution. The 2025 values are 0.00327917 and 0.00330405. The latter incremental difference is −0.00002488, with interval [−0.00009490,0.00004699]. Two additional earlier-player partitions repeat the complete inner selection. Averaging each player's loss difference across all three partitions gives −0.00003131, with conditional interval [−0.00006197,−0.00000345]. These repetitions reuse the same 41 earlier players; they are not independent studies. They strengthen the negative conclusion for this operational movement-transfer hypothesis.

Separation provides another unfavorable comparison. In the full-follow-up turn cohort, earlier nested source-demand MSE is 3.13565 squared yards and execution MSE is 3.19904. The difference interval is [−0.10311,−0.02560]. Exploratory 2025 source-demand MSE is 2.76626 and execution MSE 2.79644, with difference interval [−0.05591,−0.00286]. Later recovery is not asserted to cause separation measured at the earlier release; this is a distinct prediction from source characteristics. Using a temporal ordering that supports a plausible mechanism is necessary before any mediation or coaching-intervention claim.

Raw YAC analysis requires at least three targeted catches with measured YAC per player. It retains 17/12/11 players and 194/139/77 catches. Pass length and supplied expected YAC enter every family's context baseline. In 2025, source-demand MSE is 5.90853 and execution MSE is 4.93915, a 16.41% relative reduction with paired difference 0.96939 and interval [0.00446,2.12687]. Earlier nested execution worsens prediction: 10.92502 versus 10.70521. Removing one 2025 player at a time leaves the raw-model difference between 0.55193 and 1.16800; this is a sensitivity diagnostic and does not create independent evidence. A favorable 2025 point estimate cannot substitute for corroboration.

The meaningful reference exposes why the raw comparison is insufficient. On the same 75 catches with finite expected YAC, the supplied reference has MSE 4.94616 and raw execution MSE 4.89937. The difference is 0.04679 with a wide interval [−1.62356,1.77147]. The raw 16% improvement mainly recovers performance lost by a fitted comparator; it is not a demonstrated 16% advance over the available reference. Missing-reference catches are explicitly excluded from this paired comparison, rather than comparing MSEs from different populations.

The subsequent stronger-reference analysis fits YAC minus supplied expected YAC and gives every family the same additional zero-correction option. Its complete-reference cohort has 16/12/11 players and 183/132/75 catches. Earlier nested source-demand and execution selection both retain zero correction, exactly matching reference MSE 8.55670. Forward conventional and source-demand selection also retain the reference. The execution correction selects a forest based on 2024 validation and reaches 2025 MSE 4.86604 versus reference 4.94616: a 1.62% exploratory improvement. Its difference interval [−0.34550,0.54175] includes zero. This does not establish a substantial scouting advantage. This follow-up is explicitly exploratory after the initial YAC inspection.

Thirty training-player source-profile swaps within class preserve each donor's full profile while disrupting its match to the recipient's NFL outcomes. Under the fixed selected raw-YAC configuration, average shuffled test MSE is 5.84829 versus real-profile MSE 4.93915. One shuffled run is slightly better than the actual-profile result. This is useful attribution evidence but not a formal permutation p-value: settings are not reselected, source/body relationships are also disrupted, and the sample contains only eleven 2025 players. The entire score bank remains available. It does not override the strong-reference or earlier-player limitations.

Thirty-eight same-player, same-drill first/last pairs support more repeatable turn descriptors than some earlier sprint response-time features. Spearman correlations are 0.95382 for approach speed, 0.76146 for angle, 0.63710 for deficit, 0.67546 for retention, 0.52555 for exit ratio and 0.79757 for normalized area. These repetitions still incorporate alignment, effort and preprocessing. Repeatedly observing a characteristic establishes neither transfer to a different task nor improvement beyond conventional physical measurements.

Under the frozen first-peak definition, 2025 NFL demand overlap with earlier-class source central rectangles is only 16.92% for IN, 6.67% for OUT, 14.29% for SLANT and 2.44% for HITCH. The rectangles use marginal fifth–ninety-fifth percentiles of entry speed and angle. They do not establish biological support boundaries, and they do not prove lack of an informative signal. They show that these source observations often require extrapolation at the evaluated NFL demands. Event-detector choices, partial versus completed turns and route intent affect this comparison. A future semantically aligned full-break analysis must be declared separately; it cannot silently replace the first-peak experiment after unfavorable results.

The exact machinery now operates on actual source and NFL demand designs. Consider the supplied surrogate B(v,θ)=β₀+β₁u+β₂w+β₃uw+β₄u²+β₅w², with u=v/8 and w=θ_degrees/90. Quantize speeds to 0.1 yards/second and angles to one degree. Each source condition forms a rational observation row; each NFL condition forms a target row. For source matrix S and target matrix G, the target is exactly determined by source observations within this model precisely when G=L S for some decoder L. Otherwise the licensed PerfectPower routine supplies z with S z=0 and G z≠0. Changing β along z leaves modeled source observations unchanged while changing the modeled NFL response. This is a finite exact-arithmetic statement, not a proof that athlete physiology is quadratic.

Among the 56 evaluated players, nine source designs have full rank six; the other 47 leave NFL target directions ambiguous under the supplied surrogate. The source-rank histogram has three rank-two, three rank-three, twenty-six rank-four, fifteen rank-five and nine rank-six cases. A candidate condition grid combines speeds 3, 4.5, 6 and 7.5 yards/second with angles 30, 60, 90 and 120 degrees. One scalar observation raises rank by at most one. The algorithm constructs rank-completing subsets at exactly that minimum cardinality, then chooses among them using numerical unit-noise target amplification. Required extra observations are four for three players, three for three players, two for twenty-six players, one for fifteen players and zero for nine players: 88 additional scalar measurements in total. Every augmented target factorization is checked by rational arithmetic.

Minimum cardinality concerns the supplied six-parameter family, not a safe, effective or universally optimal athletic training regimen. Pooling route intent is deliberately optimistic; a route-specific family would require additional parameters. Rank sufficiency also does not imply noise stability. Actual sensor noise, physiological misspecification, measurement cost and feasible practice conditions remain outside the exact claim. Hypothetical response surfaces in the figure agree at the observed source condition locations and diverge elsewhere; their heights are constructed illustrations, not fitted biological measurements.

The practical conclusion is narrower than a hidden-talent discovery. Teams should require that a proposed metric be repeatable, measured at comparable demands, temporally aligned with its intended game consequence, and predictive beyond a meaningful reference. The current WR experiment exposes failures along that chain and proposes conditional additional measurement designs. It has not demonstrated reduced draft-bust risk, a causal development benefit, or a competition-winning prediction edge.
