# Movement observability: reconstructed actual-data analysis

The reconstructed experiment recovers the important scientific distinction: early NFL movement supplies considerable information about a receiver's speed one second later, while a detailed Combine sprint signature has not established an incremental scouting advantage. The supplied official data were reacquired with the account owner's authorized credential, held only in process memory. All nine SHA-256 hashes match the earlier source snapshot. The reconstruction is deliberately described as a new implementation, rather than as recovery of byte-identical lost source.

The source population comprises 174 forty-yard-dash attempts from 93 WRs. Every accepted attempt supplies 26 descriptors. The NFL analysis comprises 23, 21 and 18 rookie receivers from 2023, 2024 and 2025, with 4,764, 4,337 and 3,392 qualifying routes. It contains one fewer 2023 route than the observed older sprint experiment. There are 14,788 candidate receiver-route records before event/window and per-player filters. Exactly 1,850 records have missing or nonunique snap/release events and 338 release before 1.5 seconds. Retained windows require sixteen continuous samples, all finite positions and speeds, unique events, and at least twenty windows per player. Sample exclusion is a selection mechanism, not evidence that missing plays share the same behavior.

Write y as the mean speed at 1.3–1.5 seconds. The game observation vector contains speed at snap, mean speed at 0.3–0.5 seconds, backward-looking acceleration estimated from the two three-frame means, and distance traveled through 0.5 seconds. The future target is absolute speed; it does not subtract one of the input speeds. No frame after 0.5 seconds enters this vector. Supplied speed may nevertheless involve vendor smoothing that has not been independently characterized, and charted situation fields are not asserted to be causally available in real time. Duration eligibility also uses the later release. The experiment therefore establishes predictive information in a conditional recorded-play dataset, not a deployable live forecast.

The source speed models are s₁(t)=V[1−exp(−z)] and s₂(t)=V[1−(1+z)exp(−z)], where z=max((t−d)/τ,0). Least squares estimates V, τ and d after a seven-frame cubic Savitzky–Golay filter. Bounds are V∈[6,20], τ∈[0.1,4], d∈[−1,1]; the initialization is (12,1,−0.1), with at most 400 function evaluations. Each order supplies these three estimates, root-mean-square fit residual, and Jacobian condition number. Six fixed-time speed samples, four acceleration samples, five Legendre coefficients on the first three seconds, and the one-second/three-second speed ratio complete the dictionary. There is no assumption that the first tracked frame is a perfectly aligned biological start time. Parameters summarize observed curves, not unobserved muscle physiology.

The matched conventional predictor uses height, weight, timed tests, jump measurements, arm/hand/wingspan measurements, bench reps and supplied NGS scores, together with shared game context. Career totals and draft selection do not enter the model. The no-state comparator still includes charted football context: it is not a pure pre-draft career forecast. The comparison isolates the incremental tracking channel conditional on those supplied context variables. Each model receives five candidate settings: ridge penalties 1, 10 and 100, a 150-tree depth-six random forest, and 150-tree depth-eight extra trees. Tree leaves require twenty observations, and all trees use the same seed. Identical budgets prevent an apparent feature advantage from being merely a larger tuning search.

Source preprocessing fits on one record per training prospect. Clip each source coordinate to training 1st–99th percentiles, median-impute with missingness indicators, and standardize. A ridge map from standardized physical attributes predicts the source coordinates; retain innovations z=M−M̂(physical). The dictionary [z,z²] introduces nonlinear response structure without an unrestricted parameter explosion. Its unsupervised PCA comparator retains four or eight directions. Target-conditioned PLS retains one or two directions, trained against player-mean response innovations. Those innovations come from additional class-stratified player cross-fitting with separately trained conventional encoders. Consequently nuisance scaling and categorical encoding also exclude the held-out nuisance players. This is stricter than simply cross-fitting the regressor on globally preprocessed training rows.

Model loss is L=(1/P)Σₚ nₚ⁻¹Σⱼ(yₚⱼ−ŷₚⱼ)². Four class-stratified folds split unique earlier-season players; every route of a player stays in the same fold. Each outer training set uses three analogous inner folds to choose a candidate. A separate season-forward comparison trains on 2023, tunes on 2024, refits both and evaluates 2025. Transformations, response directions and regularization are never fitted using an outer-test or 2025 label. This algorithmic separation does not erase the broader research program's prior inspection of 2025. Those results remain exploratory, and repeated earlier-player folds do not establish generalization across wholly new seasons.

The paired player difference is 0.25605730 with conditional 95% interval [0.20672611,0.32205475]. In exploratory 2025 transport, MSE decreases from 0.97225649 to 0.64688735: a 33.4654% reduction. The difference is 0.32536914, with player interval [0.26236290,0.39855287]. Resampling sixteen team clusters while retaining the original player weights yields [0.26256926,0.40512037]. Man-coverage and zone-coverage subsets also show positive state-channel differences, but these are correlated subsets, not independent discoveries. The simple speed-persistence and constant-acceleration baselines have MSE 17.37080 and 4.27741; they are retained as sanity checks and do not replace the stronger learned conventional comparator.

The Combine increment is much smaller and uncertain. With early state present, target-conditioned motion gives 2025 MSE 0.64468264, a difference of 0.00220471 versus its conventional comparator, with interval [−0.00225527,0.00706540]. Earlier nested MSE is 0.71024781, slightly worse than conventional; the difference is −0.00089833 with interval [−0.00605799,0.00352273]. Without the game-state vector, target-conditioned source features give 2025 MSE 0.96809963 versus 0.97225649, but the difference interval [−0.00084349,0.00939094] includes zero and earlier nested prediction worsens. Unsupervised PCA comparisons are also inconclusive. These findings do not support a proven incremental Combine benefit.

Thirty whole-profile swaps among training players within class preserve each donor's source-feature relationships while disrupting the link to the recipient's outcome. The held-out profiles are unchanged, and the previously selected model setting is fixed. Average shuffled-model 2025 MSE is 0.63943023, lower than the real-profile model's 0.64468264. This does not furnish a formal permutation p-value, because tuning is not repeated for each shuffle. It does undermine a confident attribution of the small nominal improvement to the actual receiver's Combine signature. The entire shuffle bank is retained, including unfavorable evidence.

Across eighty-one receivers with repeated source attempts, first/last Spearman correlation is 0.82507 for fitted second-order capacity V, but 0.37297 for response time τ. Fixed-time speed at 1.5 seconds has correlation 0.27475, compared with 0.63259 at three seconds. This difference suggests that fine early response descriptors are less stable than later speed/capacity descriptors in the supplied recordings. Alignment, athlete effort, derivative estimation and vendor processing can all contribute; the study does not identify their separate causal effects. Repeatability is descriptive across the source population and is not used to select the predictive dictionary.
