# Exact residue algebras and the parameter boundary

The arithmetic here comes from `E8KZ.A` and `E8KZ.B` on the five invariant adjoint four-tensors, ordered by channels $1,248,3875,30380,27000$. The connection is

\[
\frac{dF}{dz}=\hbar\left(\frac A z+\frac B{z-1}\right)F,
\qquad \operatorname{spec}A=(-60,-30,-12,0,2).
\]

**The two actual residues generate the full matrix and Lie algebras.** Exact breadth-first word selection gives the unital associative algebra $M_5(\mathbb Q)$, with 25 independent words of maximum length five; the Lie algebra generated by $A,B$ has dimension 25, with maximum nested word length seven. The traceless residues $A+20I,B+20I$ generate $\mathfrak{sl}_5(\mathbb Q)$, with dimension 24. Every certificate records the actual rational matrices, construction words, pivot columns and pivot values. Exhausting the queue proves closure under multiplication or commutation with both generators; no floating rank test is used.

**A constructive proof makes the rank transparent.** The nonzero off-diagonal entries of $B$ form the bidirected graph with undirected edges $01,12,14,23,34$. Their eigenvalues under $\operatorname{ad}_A$ are $\pm30,\pm18,\pm32,\pm12,\pm2$, all distinct. Include zero for the diagonal part of $B$. For an edge eigenvalue $d$, the degree-ten polynomial

\[
p_d(X)=\prod_{e\in\{0,\pm2,\pm12,\pm18,\pm30,\pm32\}\setminus\{d\}}
\frac{X-e}{d-e}
\]

satisfies $p_d(\operatorname{ad}_A)B=B_{ij}E_{ij}$. The implementation evaluates all ten polynomials exactly. Simple graph paths and $[E_{ij},E_{jk}]=E_{ik}$ produce all twenty off-diagonal matrix units. Their opposite commutators give the four independent diagonal differences. Finally $\operatorname{tr}A=-100\ne0$ supplies the scalar direction. This proves absolute residue irreducibility as well as the ranks; it does not yet prove irreducibility of specialized monodromy.

**Selfadjointness and invariance are different equations.** The common commutant consists of scalars. The space of matrices $G$ satisfying $A^tG=GA$ and $B^tG=GB$ has dimension one, with positive representative

\[
G=\operatorname{diag}\left(1,\frac{450}{31},\frac{6125}{31},
\frac{245}{124},\frac{18375}{8}\right).
\]

The rational crossing matrix is orthogonal for this $G$. In contrast, the common solution space of $A^tG+GA=B^tG+GB=0$ is zero, and remains zero for the centered residues. These are complete exact linear solves on all 25 matrix coordinates. The positive selfadjoint residue form does not itself certify a monodromy-invariant Hermitian form: the two local monodromies must first be transported into a common basis.

**Formal generic monodromy is full $GL_5$.** Regard $\hbar$ as constant for $d/dz$. Near $\hbar=0$, nonresonant local Frobenius solutions and their connection matrix $\Phi(\hbar)$ are analytic, with $\Phi(0)=I$. Up to one common conjugation, the two local monodromies are

\[
M_0=e^{2\pi i\hbar A},\qquad
M_1=\Phi(\hbar)^{-1}e^{2\pi i\hbar B}\Phi(\hbar).
\]

All eigenvalues of $A,B$ are even integers, so if $q=e^{4\pi i\hbar}$ has infinite multiplicative order, the cyclic closure of either local monodromy is its one-dimensional integer-weight torus. Its Lie algebra contains the corresponding conjugated residue. The 25 recorded Lie words evaluated on $A,\Phi^{-1}B\Phi$ have a determinant with nonzero constant term, because the actual residue words have rank 25. Consequently their determinant is a unit over $\mathbb C[[\hbar]]$. The formal monodromy closure over the algebraic closure of $\mathbb C((\hbar))$ is therefore $GL_5$.

The same determinant is nonzero throughout some complex neighborhood of zero. Thus **there exists $\epsilon>0$** such that $0<|\hbar|<\epsilon$ and infinite-order $e^{4\pi i\hbar}$ imply complex monodromy closure $GL_5(\mathbb C)$. No numerical value of $\epsilon$ is claimed. This connection is regular singular at zero, one and infinity. Schlesinger density identifies its pointwise differential Galois group with the monodromy Zariski closure, so that group is also $GL_5$ for the stated small infinite-order specializations. The formal claim concerns algebraic Zariski closure; no parameterized Kolchin group, nor a separate generic Picard–Vessiot group over $\mathbb C(\hbar)(z)$, is computed here. In particular this is not a claim that a Zariski-open subset of all complex parameters has full $GL_5$ closure.

**Every rational specialization requires a separate argument.** Liouville's determinant formula gives

\[
\det M_0=\det M_1=e^{2\pi i\hbar\operatorname{tr}A}
=e^{-200\pi i\hbar}.
\]

For rational $\hbar$, these generate a finite determinant image of exact order the denominator of $100\hbar$. The connected monodromy closure therefore lies in $SL_5$; the full closure cannot be $GL_5$. This determinant conclusion is valid even at resonant parameters. Resonant residues, however, do not let one infer the order of local monodromy merely from its eigenvalues, since a logarithmic unipotent part may occur.

At level four, $\hbar=1/34$, the five local eigenvalues are $\zeta_{17}^{4},\zeta_{17}^{2},\zeta_{17}^{11},1,\zeta_{17}$. No nonzero integral difference of exponents occurs, so the local matrices are diagonalizable of exact order 17. The determinant image has exact order 17. The complete possible-similitude test gives a further obstruction without any continuation approximation: a bilinear form satisfying $M_0^tGM_0=\alpha G$ has entries only where $s_i+s_j=a\pmod {17}$, with $\alpha=\zeta_{17}^a$. Exhausting all 17 values of $a$ gives maximum rank three. Thus the specialized local monodromy alone excludes a nondegenerate projectively invariant complex bilinear form. This rules out a projective orthogonal explanation once an appropriate irreducible closure has been established elsewhere. It neither computes a positive Hermitian form nor proves the whole specialized group irreducible or infinite.

**Sources and proof dependencies.** The word, projection, form and spectrum results above are finite arithmetic certificates produced from this repository's E8 construction. The parameter argument is an explicit consequence of local Frobenius theory, cyclic torus closure and the recorded determinant. The regular-singular differential Galois identification is the classical Schlesinger theorem, recalled in the introduction of C. Mitschi and M. F. Singer, *Monodromy groups of parameterized linear differential equations with regular singularities* ([primary author preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/1106.2664v2), 2012), and in M. F. Singer, *Introduction to the Galois Theory of Linear Differential Equations*, Theorem 1.3.27 ([author's lecture notes](https://singer.math.ncsu.edu/papers/LMSlectures.pdf)). Their parameterized Kolchin-density theorem is a different statement and is not substituted for the pointwise or formal arguments here. Residue generation, Schlesinger density and the classical generic-parameter mechanism are established mathematics; the contribution is their explicit exact replay for this five-channel arithmetic model.
