The first 417 equation records retain their IDs and meanings. ## Start with a question

Extract the complete archive and run `python run_observatory.py`, or double-click `Start_Math_Lab.bat` on Windows. The launcher starts the loopback server and opens the page. Python 3.10 or newer is required. The first view offers five plain-language questions: what can we know, what possibilities fit, what should we measure, is a calculation reliable, and what hidden structure explains the result. Each tool has a computed example, exact printed results, its mathematical scope, expandable details and a downloadable calculation record. Available numeric and Boolean inputs change the computation; advanced requests use the same JSON router as the CLI. The page retains all earlier finite-model editing, action-history animation, cost ceilings, sensor traces, rank navigation, adaptive policies and remote series queries behind the Finite systems switch.

The animation displays returned state counts, values, branches or other computed geometry. Bars are reserved for commensurate quantities; heterogeneous metrics use labeled flow nodes. Animation can be paused or replayed, and reduced-motion preferences are respected. A moving highlight does not represent new simulated observations. All 450 equation entries can be searched by ID, family, formula, evidence status or assumptions, and rejected entries remain labeled rejected.

For an installed checkout, `python -m pip install -e .` installs the core without optional scientific libraries. Run `perfectjet lab --open` for the interface. `perfectjet math-lab --lab precision` evaluates one tool, `perfectjet math-lab --request examples/mathlab/pipeline.json` evaluates an exported request, and `perfectjet math-lab --all --output results/mathlab/all-examples.json` recomputes every default example. ## One complete decision rather than disconnected demonstrations

`workflow.decision_pipeline` accepts measurements $Hx=y$, one target loading $g_i$ per original item, nonnegative integer resource costs and capacities, and explicit deterministic observation and model bounds. It first checks whether every requested target is in the measured row space. When a target is hidden, it returns a nullspace witness and searches the supplied finite candidate sensors for a minimum-total-cost repair. It does not pretend that those extra measurements have already been obtained. An empty or inadequate candidate list returns an explicit absence of a repair within that list.

When recovery is possible, exact weights satisfy $g_i=w_i^T H$. The item value is $v_i=w_i^Ty$, and its conservative error radius is $r_i=\sum_j|w_{ij}|e_j+m_i$. A single `SelectionPopulation` then counts every feasible original portfolio, supplies its rank and inverse rank, and computes the nominal optimum with every tie. For each feasible selection $b$, the adapter computes $\hat J(b)=\sum_i b_i v_i$ and $r(b)=\sum_i b_i r_i$. A unique winner is certified only if its lower bound exceeds every rival upper bound. Shared observation errors make these simple radii conservative; interval overlap is not a proof that the underlying choice is impossible.

The default example has three items with resource vectors $(2,1),(1,2),(2,2)$, capacities $(3,3)$, measured latent values $(12,8)$ and target rows $(1,0),(0,1),(1,2)$. It has five feasible portfolios. The third item has value 28, while the first two together have value 20, giving an exact margin of eight at zero supplied error. Increasing the observation uncertainty can remove the certificate. Replacing the two measurements by their sum makes individual targets structurally hidden. These three cases distinguish a separated winner, uncertain contenders and missing information using the same input model.

The adapter permits at most fourteen original items, twelve latent coordinates, three resources and twelve candidate repair sensors. It enumerates all feasible portfolios after completion counting; it does not claim scalable arbitrary nonlinear optimization. Measurement centers must fit an exact nominal latent state, even when nonzero sensitivity bounds are supplied. General inconsistent noisy-center interval feasibility is outside this adapter. The stated bounds are caller-supplied deterministic assumptions, not fitted confidence intervals, live prices, buyer commitments or a claim of market or NFL superiority. Exact zero-error ties are reported as ties, with the optimal count and retained choices; requesting more arithmetic precision cannot break an exact tie.

## Exact Moyal and Hahn engines

`moyal.py` supplies Gaussian rational coefficients and multivariate polynomials over $\mathbb Q[i][\hbar]$. The finite star product computes $f\star g=\sum_k(i\hbar/2)^k\Lambda^k(f,g)/k!$. It also exposes Poisson brackets, the scaled Moyal commutator, Egorov defects, classification into exact, central-defect and noncentral-defect cases, polynomial pullbacks and covariance examples. Higher terms vanish when either polynomial has degree at most two. That affine-quadratic sector is a Lie subalgebra under the scaled commutator; it is not an associative star subalgebra.

For the retained convention, the cubic defect is $\Delta_\hbar(q^3,p^3)=-3\hbar^2/2$, while the quintic defect is $-150\hbar^2q^2p^2+15\hbar^4/2$. Ordinary affine symplectic substitution preserves the same flat star product. For the nonlinear symplectic shear $\Phi(q,p)=(q,p+q^2)$ and $f=p^2,g=p$, Poisson covariance holds but ordinary star covariance has exact defect $-\hbar^2/2$. Nonlinear covariance needs a transported product or corrected quantum pullback. The new module was adapted from the recovered source packet while removing its optional NumPy experiment from the supported exact core.

`hahn.py` computes the integer coefficients $S_k(a,b,c,d)=\sum_r(-1)^r\binom kr(a)_{k-r}(b)_r(c)_r(d)_{k-r}$. With $\kappa=\min(a,d)+\min(b,c)$, monomial exactness is equivalent to the vanishing of every odd $S_k$ from three through $\kappa$. The full finite gate is important: the decoy $(1,4,3r+2,2r)$ has $S_3=0$ but $S_5=720r^2(3r-1)(3r+1)(3r+2)\ne0$. The four classified $\kappa=4$ ray families and reversals are generated explicitly. The manuscript's global absence conjecture for further nonzero-Poisson corridors remains conjectural; a degree-80 census does not prove it. The tests compare the independent Hahn coefficient classifier against the exact polynomial Moyal engine and replay the classified finite sector.

## Constructive obstruction repair and reachable algebra

`categorical.py` represents cyclic phases as exact rational numbers modulo integers. It computes the cocycle $\omega_k(a,b,c)=\exp(2\pi i k a\lfloor(b+c)/n\rfloor/n)$, its class-detecting cyclic product, and the explicit cochain $B(a,b)=\exp(2\pi i k(a\bmod n)\lfloor b/n\rfloor/n)$ on $\mathbb Z/n^2$. The coboundary equation $dB=q^*\omega_k$ is replayed over every triple within the declared budget. A nontrivial class on the original cyclic group can therefore become explicitly trivial after this extension. The tool does not claim a minimum extension or an arbitrary-group repair theorem.

The same module accepts full matrix sectors and a rational self-adjoint projector. An edge joins sectors when the corresponding projector block is nonzero. The generated star algebra is a direct sum of full matrix algebras on the connected component sums, and is the full matrix algebra exactly when the sector graph is connected. Small examples independently generate the matrix algebra and compare its dimension with the graph result. Projector, symmetry and resource checks are explicit; mathematical access to a cross-sector operator is a supplied resource, not something a physical superselection rule automatically permits.

## Exact conductor transport, theta coefficients and torus products

`conductor_transport.py` implements unnormalized finite Fourier maps and chirps in exact cyclotomic quotient rings. For $M=dL$, support embedding and periodic pullback satisfy $F_M\iota_d=\pi^*F_L$, $F_M\pi^*=d\iota_dF_L$ and $T_M\iota_d=\iota_dT_L^d$. Coprime CRT factorization retains the required twists. These are maps between specified representations; a complement of an embedding is not automatically an invariant old/new subrepresentation. The normalized square-root factors are explained separately so they do not get mixed into the exact unnormalized implementation.

For an integer symmetric matrix $C$ with even diagonal, `FiniteLatticeTheta` computes $Q(a)=a^TCa/2$ and the finite numerator $N(x)=\sum_{\rho\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}^r}\zeta^{Q(\rho)}x^{|\rho|}$. Residue periodicity gives the complete series $N(x)/(1-x^L)^r$. The implementation evaluates remote coefficients, compares short direct coefficients and verifies the boundary-twisted numerator reciprocity. It does not infer modularity of an associated infinite Euler product. The torus skein API computes root-of-unity products and commutators without silently reducing curve coordinates modulo the conductor. The half-lift API implements the integer-height parity criterion at even level, with the necessary normalization restriction retained.

## Precision, finite weighted populations and outcome channels

`precision.py` treats terms as exact rationals and replays round-to-nearest, ties-to-even at a declared significand precision with arbitrary exponent range. A float denotes its stored binary value; a decimal string denotes the exact declared decimal. For a nonzero sum, $\kappa_{sum}=\sum|t_j|/|\sum t_j|$ measures cancellation. The bound $\gamma_{N-1}\kappa_{sum}$ applies under its roundoff assumptions; zero sums use absolute bounds. Evaluation, input and model errors remain separate. The module can increase precision until a stated arithmetic tolerance is met, or decline a comparison whose external uncertainty remains too large. It reproduces $10^{16}+1-10^{16}=0$ at 53-bit recursive precision and repairs the sum to one at 106 bits. It does not model IEEE underflow, overflow or exception flags.

`weighted_growth.py` supplies exact type-histogram repricing, weighted moments and covariances, finite colored path counting, chronology-sensitive edge weights, and finite nonnegative-matrix row/block tail certificates. Floating log-weight evaluations are separately labeled. A block norm can establish contraction even when a one-step row bound fails. Transfer to an external infinite path population requires an explicit weight-domination contract. Submultiplicativity can provide an infimum characterization of a growth constant; it does not make every finite root bound monotonically decrease. These distinctions prevent a convenient finite calculation from being advertised as an unrelated infinite-model theorem.

`branch_channels.py` retains exact, frame-correctable, repeat and silent-failure outcome classes with probabilities summing exactly to one. Under the declared independent identical-attempt model, failure after $R$ attempts is $p_s\sum_{j=0}^{R-1}p_r^j+p_{exhaust}p_r^R$. Expected attempt cost, acceptance-conditioned error, exhaustion and the silent floor remain distinct. The scheduler checks every allowed finite depth against a strict failure threshold and optimizes the declared cost-plus-risk objective. Increasing repetition cannot eliminate the asymptotic silent floor $p_s/(1-p_r)$ when $p_r<1$. Correction operators and device probabilities are input assumptions, and no hardware performance claim is introduced.

## Analytic replacement and arithmetic reconstruction

`analytic_recovery.py` replaces a specific tracked circular logarithmic integral by its exact rational coefficient of $2\pi i$. It validates the supplied divisor geometry, charge, loop radius, orientation and branch convention. The resulting integral depends on the representative and radius; only the logarithmic charge is the robust integer datum. The caller remains responsible for the completeness of the supplied divisor list. This computation does not claim a radius-independent period or a knot-volume theorem.

The same module implements exact cyclotomic Dirichlet characters, finite coefficients of $\sum\varphi(n)\chi(n)n^{-s}=L(s-1,\chi)/L(s,\chi)$, independent formal Dirichlet division and reduced-progression character decomposition. Correct Euler factors are retained. In particular, the residue for $n\equiv1\pmod3$ is $3/(8\zeta(2))$, rather than the unqualified $1/\zeta(2)$ sometimes written in the older prose. Exact finite coefficient tests support the implementation; the standard analytic identity uses $\Re s>2$ before continuation. Irrational phase twists remain a separate analytic problem.

`algebra_bridges.py` makes the existing quadratic compiler, supplied Pell orbit, complete finite action-history population, bounded invariant-law search and parameter-rank/Smith analysis accessible through explicit adapters. Rank cells describe algebraic rank, while an integer solution is certified at a specified specialization; constant rank does not imply constant integer feasibility. The quadratic-history adapter checks original input reconstruction and nonnegative domains before accepting an endpoint, retaining original word multiplicity.

The signed Pell asymptotic issue is corrected in `quadratic.py`. Positive embedding classes are reduced exactly into a unit fundamental interval and deduplicated; admissible reconstruction residues supply an exact rational logarithmic weight. For $n^2+7=2y^2$, the correct positive-input coefficient is $2/\log(3+\sqrt8)$, and there are 31 hits through $10^{12}$. Summing four raw signed orbit records would double that coefficient. Raw orbit records are still inspectable, and exact hit enumeration remains intact. The seeded residue adapter continues to claim only its supplied seed/unit orbit, not every norm solution.

`recovered_partners.py` restores the archived squarefree quintic partner, its factored eliminant metadata and exact active-gate replay. It also exposes universal cubic/quartic and sufficient degree-12 sextic constructions through the existing covariant engine. The full maximal-minor elimination behind the archived eliminant is not regenerated by displaying its factors; rounded complex orbit matching is not an exact orbit proof; the unrestricted degree-12 converse stays open.
