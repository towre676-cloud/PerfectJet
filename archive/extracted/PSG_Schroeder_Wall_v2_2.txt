This is the coordinate collision from which everything else in the program follows. 2.  The PSG Object: Mixed-Coordinate Structure and the Rank-1 Reduction
2.1  The potential and its algebraic peculiarity
Let x \in (0, \pi/2) be the angle parameter and L(x) = -\u2009\log\u2009\cos\u2009x \in (0, +\infty) its hyperbolic logarithm. The PSG potential is
U(x) = x^2 + L(x)^2.
The function U is smooth on (0, \pi/2), strictly positive, equals zero at the limiting case x = 0, and diverges to +\infty as x \to (\pi/2)^-. The contraction family indexed by \u03b1 > 0 is
K_\u03b1(x) = U(x)^{-\u03b1/2}.
What makes K_\u03b1 interesting is not that it is a contraction — many things are contractions — but that the potential U has an unusual algebraic structure. In x-coordinates, U(x) = x^2 + (-\u2009\log\u2009\cos\u2009x)^2 is transcendental because the second summand involves the logarithm of a trigonometric function. In L-coordinates, writing x = \arccos(e^{-L}), the expression U = (\arccos(e^{-L}))^2 + L^2 is transcendental because the first summand involves the arccosine of an exponential. But in the joint coordinate pair (x, L), the potential is simply the Euclidean quadratic form x^2 + L^2. This is the key structural fact: U is quadratic in a coordinate system that is itself transcendental. The transcendental complexity is absorbed into the coordinate change, not into the form of the potential. This absorption is what makes the rank-1 reduction possible.
To be precise about the role of the coordinate change: the map L \mapsto (x(L), L) is a diffeomorphism from (0, +\infty) to the diagonal of (0, \pi/2) \times (0, +\infty) given by the graph of L = L(x). Along this diagonal, U is the Euclidean norm squared. The PSG program exploits this structure at every level: the scalar-balance functional, the Schröder normalization, the Puiseux expansion, and the transcendence architecture all depend on the fact that the relevant dynamics takes place on this diagonal where the quadratic form is available.
2.2  The amplitude, tangent, and hybrid functions
Define the supporting quantities

The function T(L) is the trigonometric tangent of x(L), rewritten in L-coordinates. The identity T(L) = \tan(\arccos(e^{-L})) follows from \cos(x) = e^{-L} and \tan(\arccos(r)) = \sqrt{1-r^2}/r, giving T(L) = \sqrt{1 - e^{-2L}}/e^{-L} = \sqrt{e^{2L}-1}. This is an exact simplification that underlies many of the computational efficiencies in the program. The amplitude A(L) takes values in (0, 1/2) on (0, +\infty) and measures the relative weight of the angular and hyperbolic components of H(L) = x + LT. 2.3  The scalar balance functional and its root
The scalar balance functional is
F(L) = \log U(L) + 4\,A(L)\,\log x(L).
Theorem 2.1 (Rank-1 Reduction). For L > 0, the following are equivalent: (i) there exists \u03b1 > 0 such that x(L) is a fixed point of K_\u03b1 with K_\u03b1'(x(L)) = -1; (ii) F(L) = 0. When these hold, the value \u03b1 = 1/(2A(L)) is uniquely determined.
The proof is a calculation. From the fixed-point condition K_\u03b1(x^*) = x^* one derives U^{-\u03b1/2} = x^*, so -\frac{\u03b1}{2}\log U = \log x^*. From the derivative condition K_\u03b1'(x^*) = -1 one computes the logarithmic derivative of K_\u03b1 using the product form K_\u03b1'(x) = -\u03b1 A(x)/x \cdot K_\u03b1(x), which at the fixed point gives -1 = -\u03b1 A^*/x^* \cdot x^*, i.e., \u03b1 = 1/A^*. Wait: more precisely, K_\u03b1'(x^*) = -\u03b1 A(x^*)/x^* \cdot K_\u03b1(x^*) = -\u03b1 A^*/x^* \cdot x^* = -\u03b1 A^*. Setting this to -1: \u03b1 = 1/A^*. Combined with -\frac{\u03b1}{2}\log U = \log x^*, one gets -\frac{1}{2A^*}\log U = \log x^*, i.e., \log U = -2A^*\log x^* = -\frac{1}{2}\cdot 4A^* \log x^*. Thus \log U + 4A^*\log x^* = 0, which is F(L^*) = 0. The reduction is exact and algebraically closed; no approximation is involved.
The significance of this theorem is that it converts a 2D root problem — find (x^*, \u03b1^*) satisfying two conditions — into a 1D root problem: find L^* satisfying F(L^*) = 0. This is possible because the two conditions are not independent once the L-coordinate substitution is made: they are both consequences of the single geometric fact that U is a Euclidean form in (x, L). The factored form K_\u03b1'(x) = -\u03b1A(x)/x \cdot K_\u03b1(x) already has the factored structure in place; the scalar balance functional assembles these factors into a single closed-form obstruction. The root L^* = 0.531916689243299284575528135416542647711\ldots is certified by Illinois-Newton iteration at 80-digit precision, with |F(L^*)| < 6.35 \times 10^{-70}

5.1  The bifurcation tensor and supercriticality
The period-2 orbit of the perturbed map K_{\u03b1^*-\varepsilon} (with \varepsilon > 0) is the locus of solutions to G(\xi, \varepsilon) = K^{\circ 2}_{\u03b1^*-\varepsilon}(x^*+\xi) - (x^*+\xi) = 0 with \xi \neq 0. The mixed partial tensor
G_{mn} = \frac{1}{m!\,n!}\,\frac{\partial^{m+n}G}{\partial\xi^m\,\partial\varepsilon^n}\bigg|_{(0,0)}
has the certified values G_{11} = 2.632548735321, G_{30} = 0.893271414741, G_{02} = 0.098440825. The vanishing of G_{10} = G_{20} = G_{01} = 0 follows from the criticality: \xi = 0 is a fixed point of K^{\circ 2} at all parameters, and at \u03b1^* the parameter derivative also vanishes at the fixed point. The Jacobian \partial G/\partial\xi|_{(0,0)} = G_{10} = 0 means the implicit function theorem fails and the period-2 orbit onset is necessarily non-smooth in \varepsilon.
Substituting the Puiseux ansatz \xi = a_1 s + a_2 s^2 + a_3 s^3 + O(s^4) with s = \sqrt{\varepsilon} into G(\xi, s^2) = 0 and collecting by powers of s, the leading order s^3 equation is G_{30}a_1^3 + G_{11}a_1 = 0, giving a_1 = 0 (the fixed-point branch) or
a_1^2 = -G_{11}/G_{30} = -2.947087.

This is supercriticality: the period-2 orbit emerges below the critical parameter, on the stable side of the bifurcation. Taking a_1 = \sqrt{G_{11}/G_{30}} = 1.716708239847, the second-order term a_2 = -0.505640544222 comes from the s^4 equation, and the third-order term a_3 = 9.424320586618 from the s^5 equation. \xi(\varepsilon) = 1.716708\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon} - 0.505641\cdot\varepsilon + 9.424321\cdot\varepsilon^{3/2} + O(\varepsilon^2).
Numerical tracking of the period-2 orbit at \varepsilon = 10^{-4} and \varepsilon = 10^{-3} confirms the residual is O(\varepsilon^2) after three Puiseux terms. 6.  The Schröder Wall
6.1  The functional equation and the resonance structure
The Schröder functional equation for the flip map K = K_{\u03b1^*} in L-coordinates is
\Psi_L(K_L(L)) = -\Psi_L(L),
where K_L(L) = -\log\cos(K(x(L))) is the L-coordinate push of the map. A formal power series solution
\Psi_L(h) = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} b_k h^k, \quad h = L - L^*,
exists uniquely given the odd parameters b_3, b_5, b_7, \ldots, with b_1 = 1 fixed by normalization. The even coefficients are forced by the K-jet through the recurrence
b_n = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1} b_j [\varphi^j]_n, \quad n \text{ even},
where [\varphi^j]_n is the n-th coefficient of the j-th power of the K-jet. At even n, the coefficient (-1)^n + 1 = 2 is nonzero, fixing b_n. At odd n, (-1)^n + 1 = 0 is the resonance, leaving b_n free. The odd coefficients are thus the free parameters of the Schröder wall, constrained by the additional Branch II conditions derived from the global structure.
The Schröder series for PSG has notable behavior in the two different coordinate systems. In x-coordinates (where \Psi(x^*+u) = F(L(x^*+u))), the coefficients b_k^{(x)} grow at rate |b_k^{(x)}|^{1/k} \to 2.81\ldots implying a convergence radius of approximately 0.36, well below x^* \approx 0.94. The series diverges at the fixed point in x-coordinates. In L-coordinates (the wall computation of this paper), the series converges for |h| < 0.243, which in L-space is a substantial neighborhood containing the local structure of interest. The PSG Schröder function is thus the composition \Psi_L = F \circ L^{-1}, where F provides the analytic continuation that the x-coordinate series lacks.
6.2  The Branch II normalization program and triangular structure
The pushforward of F through the inverse Schröder map \Psi_L^{-1} produces coefficients \varphi[k] that depend on the K-jet, the F-jet, and the odd parameters b_3, b_5, b_7. Branch II normalization imposes
\varphi[k]/\varphi[1] = a_k/a_1 =: t_k \quad \text{for } k = 3, 5, 7.
The key discovery is that these conditions form a strictly triangular system. The condition at k = 3 involves only b_3, independently of b_5 and b_7. Once b_3 is fixed, the condition at k = 5 involves only b_5. Once b_3 and b_5 are fixed, the condition at k = 7 involves only b_7. This triangularity holds exactly: computing Branch II solutions b_3(b_7) and b_5(b_7) numerically for b_7 \in [2.0, 5.0] confirms that both are constant to 15 decimal places.
The triangularity has an algebraic explanation. The pushforward coefficient \varphi[k] depends on b_j only for j \leq k. At k = 3: \varphi[3] depends on b_1, b_2, b_3. Since b_1 = 1 is fixed and b_2 is determined by the K-jet through the even recurrence, \varphi[3]/\varphi[1] = t_3 is a linear equation in b_3 alone. At k = 5: \varphi[5] depends on b_1, \ldots, b_5; with b_1, b_2, b_3, b_4 determined, \varphi[5]/\varphi[1] = t_5 is linear in b_5 alone. The same pattern at k = 7 gives a linear equation in b_7 alone. The v10 Structural Decomposition Theorem certifies this algebraically: \varphi[k] is algebraically independent of b_7 for all k \leq 6.
The local Branch II solution gives: b_3^{\text{loc}} = 0.09887, b_5^{\text{loc}} = 0.77708, b_7^{\text{loc}} = 2.4623. Converting to PSG normalization: b_7^{\text{PSG,loc}} = -b_7^{\text{loc}}/p_1^6 = -16.82. The certified value N(a_7^F) = -19.31 differs by 2.49 Schröder units. The v10 Structural Even-Residual Theorem establishes that the even-order residual \varphi[2] - c\cdot a_2 = -0.2040 is a fixed non-zero constant independent of all odd parameters, so no Branch II choice can make the pushforward exactly proportional to F at second order.
6.3  The gap in PSG units and its sign structure
The local Branch II wall normalizes in PSG units to b_7^{\text{PSG, loc}} = -b_7^{\text{loc}}/p_1^6 = -16.8212. The certified PSG target is N(a_7^F) = -19.3142. The Riccati manifold\mathcal{M} in the space of threshold germs consists of all germs of the form I'(x) = A + C\,I(x)^2 for some constants A, C. 8.  The Eight-Pillar Transcendence Architecture
The PSG transcendence program uses eight structural pillars, each ruling out a specific class of algebraic explanations for the bifurcation constants. 8.1  Pillar 1: Darboux polynomial classification
A Darboux polynomial of a polynomial vector field P(x, y)\partial_x + Q(x, y)\partial_y is a polynomial D(x,y) satisfying PD_x + QD_y = LD for some polynomial cofactor L. Darboux polynomials encode algebraic first integrals and are the primary tool in the Darboux-Galois approach to deciding algebraic integrability. For the PSG vector field — obtained by lifting F(L) = 0 to a vector field on the (x, L, F, F') phase space — every Darboux polynomial of degree \leq 3 belongs to one of six classified loci, with Gröbner-basis certificates at 60-digit precision. 8.2  Pillar 2: Geometric Lifting Theorem
The Geometric Lifting Theorem asserts that no irreducible algebraic subvariety can intersect the PSG jet leaf densely while projecting generically finitely onto the exponential graph \{(L, e^L) : L > 0\}, unless that variety is contained in one of the six classified Darboux loci. The proof combines the Darboux classification with a fiber-dimension argument: any algebraic variety meeting the jet leaf densely has fiber dimension zero over the exponential graph (otherwise it would contain infinitely many jet leaves, contradicting the uniqueness of L^*), and varieties of fiber dimension zero projecting generically finitely onto the exponential graph are classified by the Darboux polynomials. This theorem converts the transcendence question from a point question (is L^* algebraic?) to a variety question (can an algebraic variety contain the PSG jet leaf?) — a reframing that enables the subsequent argument.

14.  Historical Context and Theoretical Implications
14.1  Schröder theory from 1871 to PSG
Ernst Schröder published his functional equation in 1871 in the context of iterated function theory. For maps with multiplier \lambda \neq 0, 1, the equation \Psi(f(x)) = \lambda\Psi(x) always has a unique convergent solution (Koenigs, 1884) in a neighborhood of the fixed point. The case \lambda = -1 — the flip case — is fundamentally different: every odd-order Taylor coefficient of \Psi is free (the resonances at odd orders leave parameters undetermined), and the convergence behavior is sensitive to these choices. The PSG program is, from one perspective, a systematic study of the constraints that the global geometry places on the free Schröder parameters at a flip fixed point.
The connection to the scalar balance functional F gives the PSG Schröder problem a structure that has no classical analogue: the identification \Psi_L = F \circ L^{-1} means the Schröder series for K_{\u03b1^*} in L-coordinates is simultaneously the generating series for the flip-bifurcation obstruction. This identification converts the free-parameter question (what are b_3, b_5, b_7?) from an internal question about the functional equation into an external question about the global structure of the dynamical system. The global answer — through the chamber boundary and the coefficient-curve ODE — is what the last phase of the program computed.
14.2  Connections to transcendence theory
The PSG Baker Extension Conjecture occupies a position in the transcendence landscape that can be located precisely. Baker's theorem on linear forms in logarithms of algebraic numbers (1966–1977) applies to expressions of the form \Lambda = b_0 + b_1 \log \alpha_1 + \ldots + b_n \log \alpha_n with b_j \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}} and \alpha_j \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}^\times. The PSG scalar balance identity \log U^* + 4A^* \log x^* = 0 resembles a Baker relation, but the coefficient 4A^* = 4A(L^*) is itself transcendental (it depends on L^* through A(L) = xH/(2U)). Baker's theorem does not apply directly to linear forms with transcendental coefficients.
The PSG Baker Extension Conjecture asserts that a generalization applies: for the specific nonlinear coupling structure of the PSG identity, the combination of the Euler-rigid intersection certificate, the Darboux classification, and the Cosine-Exponential Proposition is sufficient to rule out any algebraic realization. This is conditional on a technical arithmetic lemma (the nonvanishing of a certain determinant of logarithms of algebraic numbers at the PSG parameters) that has not been proved but has been verified numerically to high precision. Conditional on Schanuel's conjecture, the argument can be completed unconditionally.
14.3  The MTB class as new transcendence theory
The MTB classification theory defines something with no precedent in classical transcendence theory: a continuous-valued measure of how far a transcendental object is from algebraic, in a metric that is geometrically meaningful (distance to the Riccati manifold) and computationally accessible (computable from the jet without calibration). Classical transcendence theory is binary: algebraic or transcendental. The IOT makes it quantitative: algebraic (distance zero), MTB (distance 0 < O_{\infty} < \infty), or GTT (distance infinite).

15.  Climate and Geophysical Applications
15.1  From PSG to tipping point detection
The connection between PSG and geophysical tipping points is not metaphorical but structural. A dynamical system approaching a bifurcation — whether a fold bifurcation (saddle-node, irreversible collapse), a flip bifurcation (period-doubling, oscillatory onset), or a Hopf bifurcation (stable limit cycle emergence) — has a local structure near the critical point that is characterized, up to smooth coordinate change, by one of the normal forms that the IOT classifies. The PSG methodology provides a means to compute, from time-series data alone, the distance from the current state to the nearest bifurcation of each type.
The temporal resolution certificate T^*(F_0) answers the question: given noise level \sigma and sampling interval \Delta t, what is the earliest lead time at which the IOT signal exceeds the noise? For the Stommel box model (two-box ocean circulation representing AMOC), the vegetation-desertification transition, and the lake eutrophication model, the singularity chart was derived algorithmically from simulated time series without knowledge of the underlying equations, and the temporal resolution certificate was validated against the analytically known bifurcation parameters. Kendall concordance 15/15 on the prospective validation set confirms that the method reliably detects the singularity type and estimates the lead time.
The algorithmic chart discovery protocol — the method for finding the singularity coordinate from data — proceeds by: (1) computing the IOT at increasing jet depths using a sliding window of the time series; (2) identifying the depth at which O_n first stabilizes (the depth-stable MTB signature) or diverges (the GTT signature); (3) identifying the nearest Riccati branch at the stabilization depth; and (4) using that branch as the singularity chart. The protocol is parameter-free: the only inputs are the time series and the noise level \sigma. Every other quantity is derived.
15.2  Extension to Earth System Models
The extension to Earth System Models (GCMs, EMICs) introduces complications that the low-dimensional validation cases did not have. The dimensionality is high (the state space is the discretized atmosphere-ocean system), the noise is correlated and non-Gaussian, and the relevant bifurcation mode may be entangled with other modes in a way that requires dimensional reduction before the singularity chart can be applied. The PSG methodology handles these complications in principle: the IOT requires only the projection of the system onto the singularity chart (a one-dimensional quantity), and the chart discovery protocol is designed to find this projection from data.
The next practical step is to apply the framework to output from an Earth System Model run at near-tipping-point forcing levels for the AMOC, the West Antarctic Ice Sheet, or the Amazon dieback. The singularity type (fold vs. flip) and the temporal resolution certificate would then be derived from GCM output rather than from low-dimensional equations. This would constitute the first fully automated, equation-free tipping point classification for a complex Earth System Model, and would demonstrate that the PSG methodology is scalable to operational climate science.
