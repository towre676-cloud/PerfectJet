I. From the first lift to the second problem: extraction is not the end

The first Shadow-Core monograph established the essential separation: the abelian shadow of 5_2 is not false, but it is too small to carry the non-abelian trace field. This sequel begins one step later. The new question is not whether the discriminant--23 field is real, nor whether it appears in the character variety, nor whether the abelian shadow misses it. Those points are now the base layer. The more valuable question is what the discovery lets us compute that we could not compute cleanly before. A core field that merely names a manifold is a classification datum. A core field that supports exact coefficient arithmetic, exact saddle separation, regulator evaluation, p-adic depth, and search heuristics is a computational substrate. The second pass therefore shifts from recognition to production. It asks what kind of machine should exist once the non-abelian core has been extracted.

The answer I want to develop is that the core should be treated as an execution ring. Instead of storing a perturbative coefficient as a floating complex number, store it as an element of K = Q[u]/(u^3 - 4u^2 + 5u - 1), or equivalently Q[x]/(x^3 - x^2 + 1). Instead of deciding saddle membership by numerical proximity, decide it by elimination into exact components, then evaluate the selected component under the required embedding. Instead of searching corner-divisor coincidences by volume alone, search quotient rings for exact divisibility by candidate field avatars. Instead of treating p-adic ramification as a decorative afterthought, make it a branch-selection and denominator-control signal. The computational leap is not that exact algebra replaces numerical analysis. The leap is that exact algebra becomes the coordinate system in which numerical analysis is allowed to happen.

The machine-precision pass that precedes this document checked the small but load-bearing facts. The polynomials x^3 - x^2 + 1, x^3 - x - 1, z^3 - 2z^2 + 3z - 1, and z^3 - 4z^2 + 5z - 1 all have discriminant -23 and are irreducible over Q; hence each non-square cubic presentation has S_3 Galois closure. The Nahm free-direction cubic z^3 - 2z^2 - z + 1 has discriminant 49 and is irreducible, hence it is cyclic rather than S_3, exactly matching the conductor-7 abelian shadow. The one-loop expression (-2u^2+3u-2)/(-u^2+u), where u^3 - 4u^2 + 5u - 1 = 0, has resultant minimal polynomial y^3 + 4y^2 - 23y + 23 and discriminant -1127 = -23 * 7^2. The two-loop expression (6u-8)/(3u^2 - 8u + 5) has resultant polynomial 23y^3 + 4y + 200. Thus the perturbative coefficients are not merely numerically close to the field; they are generated by exact rational functions of the core coordinate. In the Bloch regulator check, the complex root of x^3 - x^2 + 1 produced D(z)=0.94270736277692772092129960309221164759032710576688 and 3D(z)=2.8281220883307831627638988092766349427709813173006. The translated AK root of z^3 - 4z^2 + 5z - 1 produced D=0.47135368138846386046064980154610582379516355288344, so 2D is the preceding value. These are not proof of volume conjectures, but they are high-precision sanity checks on the regulator branch.

The corner computation gave the most interesting beyond-core result. In the family P_{a,b}(z)=(1-z)^a - z^b, the visible discriminant--23 corner field z^3 - 2z^2 + 3z - 1 divides only the coprime corner (a,b)=(3,2) through a<=300 in an exact quotient-ring divisibility scan. The AK/hidden avatar z^3 - 4z^2 + 5z - 1 divides only (a,b)=(5,1) through the same scan. This means the known recurrence of the field in the corner family is not a broad periodic event at small height. It looks isolated: one direct corner and one hidden cofactor under the elementary regular-tetrahedron factor. That isolation is a new computational clue. The field is not simply recurring whenever a+b has some congruence property; the regular tetrahedron factor z^2 - z + 1 is governed by the simple rule 6 divides a+b, while the -23 cubic is not governed by that elementary cyclotomic rule in the scanned range. The non-abelian core resists the easy periodic mechanism.

This motivates the sequel thesis: a core-aware computation system should not begin by assuming that the hidden field is frequent. It should assume the opposite. The non-abelian core is sparse, and therefore valuable. It appears when a projection has become singular, when an abelian boundary branch has split off, when an exact elimination has exposed a Morse hyperbolic component, or when a corner cofactor escapes the cyclotomic rule. The computational goal is to build the extractor that finds those events without confusing them with the abundant shadows around them. In the first pass, the slogan was 7 is the shadow and -23 is the core. In this pass, the slogan becomes: compute in the core, search by the obstruction, and let the shadow serve as a routing index rather than a final answer.

II. Plate A: the Shadow-Core extractor as an algorithm, not a metaphor

The first plate in the sequel is an algorithmic plate. Given a mathematical object X presented by a knot, a state sum, a q-series, a gluing system, a corner equation, or a finite conductor module, define a pipeline with four explicit stages. The first stage computes Shadow(X), the largest reliably available abelian or finite-conductor projection. The second stage computes Obstruct(X), a proof that the target core is either contained in the shadow or cannot be contained there. The third stage constructs Receive(X), a legal non-abelian receiving space: character variety, AK critical ideal, A-polynomial branch, Bloch group class, p-adic skeleton, or exact transvectant-rank locus. The fourth stage computes Output(X), not as a decimal but as a field-valued or regulator-valued object. The extracted form is

X |-> (Shadow(X), Obstruct(X), Receive(X), Output(X)).

For 5_2 the shadow stage is almost embarrassingly rich: determinant 7, Fox-coloring spike, Alexander value at -1, linking-form Gauss sum, cyclotomic rapidity limit, and the abelian Nahm saddle Q(sqrt2) times Q(zeta_7)^+. The obstruction stage is twofold: the cyclotomic route is blocked by Kronecker-Weber because the target has S_3 Galois closure, and the Nahm-sum route is blocked by direct saddle calculation because its fields are abelian. The receiving stage is the full Andersen-Kashaev/character critical ideal, whose exact elimination gives (z-1)^2(z^3 - 4z^2 + 5z - 1). The output stage includes the Bloch-Wigner volume, the one-loop Hessian determinant, the two-loop vertex ratio, and the p-adic denominator/ramification behavior.

This is already a program. It can be implemented with exact algebra kernels and high-precision embeddings. The important design rule is that no stage is allowed to overwrite the previous one. The determinant 7 is not replaced by -23; it is stored as a shadow coordinate. The cyclotomic obstruction is not an error; it is stored as a denied-containment proof. The parabolic branch (z-1)^2 is not thrown away; it is stored as the abelian boundary component. The cubic branch is not just selected numerically; it is stored as the core component, together with discriminant, Galois marker, embedding choices, regulator values, and perturbative coefficient ring. This produces a data object that can answer questions of the form: which invariants were visible before the lift, which were impossible before the lift, and which new computations become possible after the lift?

The extractor should therefore be represented not as a single solver but as a typed graph. Nodes are rings, fields, varieties, saddles, regulators, and q-series presentations. Edges are projections, degenerations, eliminations, inclusions, obstructions, and embeddings. A shadow edge lowers arithmetic complexity: for example, character variety to abelianization, trace field to determinant, full state integral to Nahm-sum saddle, or non-abelian Galois closure to cyclotomic field if possible. A core edge raises arithmetic complexity legally: for example, cyclotomic obstruction to character variety, determinant shadow to trace-field search, Nahm det-zero matrix to full AK critical ideal. The graph has a simple health condition: every lift edge must be justified by an obstruction at the lower level. Otherwise the system merely wanders upward, adding complexity without need.

The 5_2 graph teaches a general rule for computation: the shadow is best used as a hash, not as a certificate of identity. Determinant 7 narrows the search; it does not identify the core. Q(zeta_7)^+ tells us that the Nahm free direction is linked to determinant 7; it does not imply that the full quantum field is cyclic. The same caution applies more generally. A field, determinant, or volume can look compatible with a manifold while failing the invariant trace-field test. A low-rank matrix can look like a geometric presentation while being a stabilized q-series presentation instead. A root-of-unity degeneration can look like it approaches quantum content while remaining trapped in the abelian closure. The extractor must keep each such datum in the correct lane.

Computationally, the first implementation should expose four primitives. The primitive abelian_shadow(X) returns determinant, first homology, cyclotomic support, torsion characters, and any finite conductor closure. The primitive obstruction_test(shadow, target) tests Galois containments, discriminant supports, norm denominators, and rank/kernel constraints. The primitive core_eliminate(X) computes resultants, primary components, critical equations, or field-recognized shape factors. The primitive coefficient_ring(core) constructs exact quotient arithmetic and embedding maps for numerical evaluation. The return type is not a theorem text but a computable object: a small database of exact polynomials, field embeddings, denied containments, regulator values, and coefficient vectors. That object can then generate the prose theorem, the plate, the perturbative series, or the next search.

This is the main computing breakthrough suggested by the second pass. Do not build another all-purpose symbolic solver. Build a lift extractor specialized to the repeated failure pattern: abelian data is easy and abundant, non-abelian core data is sparse and productive, and the right algorithm is the one that knows when to stop trusting the shadow. In ordinary numerical workflows, the saddle is a complex point and the coefficient is a complex number. In the core-aware workflow, the saddle is a prime ideal or irreducible factor; the coefficient is a quotient-ring element; the complex number is only an embedding of the exact object. That change of representation is the difference between estimating a phenomenon and computing in its natural field.

III. Plate B: the coefficient-field virtual machine

The second plate is the most practical. Once the AK/character saddle has been selected, every perturbative computation should be performed in the coefficient field rather than in floating arithmetic. For 5_2 choose the core coordinate u satisfying

u^3 - 4u^2 + 5u - 1 = 0.

Then every algebraic coefficient in the first computed loop data is represented by a vector a0 + a1 u + a2 u^2 with rational entries. Multiplication is reduction modulo u^3 = 4u^2 - 5u + 1. Division is inversion in the quotient field, implemented by an extended Euclidean calculation or by solving a 3 by 3 rational system. Embedding into C occurs only at the output boundary, by choosing the hyperbolic root u = 1.877438833123346... + 0.744861766619744... i. The one-loop determinant is not first a decimal. It is

H_1(u) = (-2u^2 + 3u - 2)/(-u^2 + u),

and the two-loop vertex ratio is

H_2(u) = (6u - 8)/(3u^2 - 8u + 5).

The exact resultant calculation gives minpoly(H_1)=y^3 + 4y^2 - 23y + 23 and minpoly(H_2)=23y^3 + 4y + 200. The first discriminant is -1127 = -23 * 7^2. This is a compact signature of the whole lift: the non-abelian core discriminant -23 remains visible, while the determinant/conductor shadow 7 reappears squared in the one-loop element. The coefficient field therefore remembers both the core and the shadow without confusing them.

A coefficient-field virtual machine, or CFVM, would make this exact. Its memory cells are not floats but field vectors. A cell in K_23 stores (a0,a1,a2; denominator) together with a field tag. The field tag contains the defining polynomial, discriminant, preferred embeddings, ramified primes, and known isomorphisms to alternate avatars. Arithmetic instructions are ADD, MUL, INV, EMBED_sigma, TRACE, NORM, MINPOLY, and REGULATOR_EVAL. A perturbative instruction layer then builds Feynman-like contractions, Hessian determinants, cubic and quartic vertex combinations, and rational functions of saddle coordinates. The result is a field element, and only at the last stage is it sent to a numerical embedding. This is how one avoids losing the exact coefficient field behind a fog of high-precision decimals.

The most immediate benefit is compression of perturbative expansions. A naive expansion stores c_0, c_1, c_2, ... as complex numbers with many digits. A core-aware expansion stores the field K, the selected embedding sigma, and each coefficient as a rational vector in the basis 1,u,u^2. If denominators are controlled by the ramified set {23} plus small structural primes and determinant factors, the series becomes a sparse exact object. For a long perturbative expansion, the storage model changes from O(N * precision) digits to O(N * degree * rational_height) plus one field descriptor. More importantly, equality testing becomes exact. Two coefficients are equal if their rational vectors match after reduction; two expansions match if their field tags and coefficient vectors match up to the declared field isomorphism. This is not merely more elegant. It is how one builds a reliable database of quantum invariants without silently merging near-equal but distinct embeddings.

The second benefit is automatic branch discipline. A field can have several embeddings. For the cubic K_23 there is one real embedding and one pair of complex embeddings. The hyperbolic branch requires the complex embedding with positive imaginary part. The real embedding may be useful for regulator or unit computations but is not the geometric volume branch. A CFVM can carry embedding labels explicitly. The output D(sigma(u)) is then not just D(u); it is D evaluated at the selected sigma_C^+ branch. This matters in exactly the places where ordinary numerical workflows become fragile: branch cuts of logarithms, dilogarithm sheets, Chern-Simons normalizations, Stokes walls, and p-adic Hensel branches. If the embedding is part of the type, the program is less likely to compare numbers produced by incompatible branches.

The third benefit is modularity across avatars. The Condensoroid field x^3 - x^2 + 1, the AK field z^3 - 4z^2 + 5z - 1, and the plastic avatar x^3 - x - 1 should be registered as the same field with explicit generator maps. Translation z = x + 1 maps x^3 - x^2 + 1 to z^3 - 4z^2 + 5z - 1. Inversion beta = -1/x maps the Condensoroid generator to the plastic unit. The corner polynomial z^3 - 2z^2 + 3z - 1 is a different generator of the same field. A CFVM should not duplicate these as unrelated coefficient rings. It should build a FieldID object that stores the reduced absolute polynomial, discriminant, signature, and isomorphism maps. Then when a coefficient from the AK expansion and a regulator from the Condensoroid root are compared, the system knows they live in the same arithmetic object.

The fourth benefit is that the p-adic side becomes available to the same machine. If a coefficient denominator is a power of 23, this is not a nuisance; it is a signal. The field K_23 has ramification at 23. The symmetrized Habiro series lands in Z[z,1/23]. The p-adic regulator depth in the atlas was uniformly three across the rows checked. A CFVM can record denominator support, valuation depth, and Hensel branch. Then the same coefficient object can be evaluated in C for volume, in Q_23 for ramification/skeleton behavior, and in exact algebra for identity checks. This is what it means for the core field to be the coefficient field of quantum perturbation theory: it is not only the field in which c_i happen to lie, but the field in which the entire calculation should be organized.

If built, the CFVM would be a tangible computational deliverable. It would accept a critical ideal and a chosen irreducible factor; construct the quotient field; reduce saddle variables into that quotient; compute Hessians and higher derivatives symbolically; output loop coefficients as exact field vectors; and provide controlled embeddings for numerical checks. The current computations already contain the first nontrivial test case. The one-loop and two-loop rational functions reduce exactly to cubic minimal polynomials tied to -23. The next frontier is not to restate that fact but to compute c_2, c_3, and higher terms directly in K_23, compare them against numerical state-integral asymptotics when available, and measure whether coefficient heights obey a hidden recurrence or denominator law. That would turn the discovered field into a working arithmetic processor.

IV. Plate C: exact saddle separation and branch typing

The third plate is the saddle plate. The AK critical ideal of 5_2 factors into a degenerate abelian branch and a non-abelian hyperbolic branch. The exact factorization is

F(z) = (z - 1)^2 (z^3 - 4z^2 + 5z - 1).

This is a computational blessing because it converts analytic ambiguity into algebraic typing. The factor (z - 1)^2 is not a failed approximation to the hyperbolic saddle. It is a separate component: parabolic, reducible, abelian, zero volume, non-Morse. The cubic factor is also not simply the other root. It is irreducible, discriminant -23, S_3, positive-volume, Morse in the computed Hessian sense. The branch selector can therefore begin with exact factor membership rather than a floating initial condition.

The next frontier is to formalize a branch type system. A branch B of a critical ideal receives attributes: dimension, multiplicity, field, Galois marker, volume value, Hessian status, and shadow/core flag. For the parabolic branch, B_par has field Q, multiplicity two, volume zero, Hessian singular or divergent, shadow flag abelian. For the hyperbolic branch, B_hyp has field K_23, multiplicity one, nonzero Bloch-Wigner volume, finite nonzero Hessian, core flag non-abelian. This branch type system is what lets a computation refuse to average over branches that are analytically distinct. A numerical solver may see two nearby critical behaviors or a singular limit; the branch type system knows one is a degenerate boundary component and one is the geometric saddle.

This matters for Stokes analysis. The full Stokes graph proof of contour dominance is still named open in the source work. But not every part of saddle selection has to wait for the full Stokes theorem. Exact branch typing can already remove the parabolic branch from the standard Gaussian list because it is non-Morse and zero-volume. It can certify that the hyperbolic branch is the only branch among the exact components that has the right Morse/nonzero-volume profile for a standard semiclassical contribution. That is not the same as proving dominance on the actual Faddeev contour, but it shrinks the analytic problem. The Stokes problem becomes: prove that the actual contour intersects or flows to the already-typed hyperbolic Morse branch with the required orientation and dominance. It no longer has to discover the branch from scratch.

A computational Stokes program can now be designed around exact algebra plus numerical flow. First, compute the critical ideal and factor it. Second, attach exact branch types. Third, choose a potential V and compute Re(V/hbar) and Im(V/hbar) under the selected complex embedding. Fourth, integrate steepest descent/ascent flows using high precision, but with branch endpoints anchored by exact algebraic points. Fifth, track logarithm and dilogarithm branches by continuous lifting rather than by local principal-value guesses. Sixth, compute intersection numbers of the original contour with the Lefschetz thimbles. The exact branch data supplies the endpoints; numerical flow supplies the geometry; homological intersection supplies the final saddle coefficient.

The important computational insight is that branch typing should occur before Stokes numerics. Too often the workflow is reversed: numerical saddle search first, algebraic recognition later. That risks recognizing a wrong branch because floating logs and dilogarithms can cross cuts. A core-aware system should first produce the exact branch inventory. For each branch it should know the field and degeneracy status. Only then should it run contour transport. The branch inventory becomes a map for the numerical integrator.

For 5_2, the typed branch inventory has an especially clean interpretation. The degenerate branch is the abelian shadow. The cubic branch is the non-abelian core. The equation itself contains the lift: (z-1)^2 is the boundary component that the shadow can see; z^3 - 4z^2 + 5z - 1 is the core that the shadow cannot contain. The AK critical ideal therefore does not merely host the core field; it displays the shadow-core split internally. This is precisely the sort of algebraic pattern one should search for in other knots and state integrals. The desired pattern is not only an irreducible factor with an interesting discriminant. The desired pattern is a factorization in which an abelian degenerate component and a non-abelian Morse component coexist, with the non-abelian component carrying the perturbative coefficient field.

A useful invariant for future computation is the branch lift index. Define L(B) to be zero for rational/parabolic/zero-volume branches, one for abelian cyclotomic or torsion branches, and two for non-abelian hyperbolic Morse branches with nonzero Bloch regulator. This is not a topological invariant yet; it is a computational classifier. For the 5_2 AK ideal, L((z-1)^2)=0 and L(cubic)=2. For the 4_1 ideal tetrahedron calibration, the geometric field Q(sqrt(-3)) is abelian but volume-positive, so it occupies a special torsion-regulator class: L=1 with geometry. That distinction is valuable. It separates abelian geometry from non-abelian geometry, rather than flattening every hyperbolic volume into the same bucket. The goal is not to worship non-abelian data but to know exactly which computations require it.

The plate should therefore depict a branch table, not a single arrow. Branches are rows; columns are exact factor, field, discriminant, Galois type, regulator, Hessian, and status. The 5_2 row with discriminant -23 is then the first complete non-abelian core branch. The next computational target is to populate this table for a family: twist knots, small census manifolds, corner-divisor pins, or state-integral presentations. A breakthrough would be a classifier that predicts from the shadow whether a non-abelian Morse branch must appear, and then finds it by exact elimination with minimal search.

V. Plate D: the corner-divisor search as a real discovery engine

The fourth plate turns the corner-divisor family into a search engine. The family is small enough to be exact and large enough to reveal structure:

P_{a,b}(z) = (1-z)^a - z^b, gcd(a,b)=1, a>b>=1.

The first pass used it as a source of trace-field pins: (3,2) gives the 5_2 field; (3,1) gives the -31 field; (4,1) and (4,3) give the -283 quartic field. The second-pass computation asks a more targeted question: is the discriminant--23 cubic a periodic resident of the corner family, or is it sparse? To avoid volume numerics, the scan worked in quotient rings. For a candidate cubic g(z), compute (1-z)^a and z^b modulo g(z); if they are equal, then g divides P_{a,b}. This avoids factoring every high-degree P_{a,b}; it tests exact divisibility in the field itself.

For g_1(z)=z^3 - 2z^2 + 3z - 1, the visible corner avatar, the exact scan through a<=300 found only (a,b)=(3,2). For g_2(z)=z^3 - 4z^2 + 5z - 1, the AK/translated avatar, the exact scan through a<=300 found only (a,b)=(5,1). This does not prove global uniqueness, but it is strong evidence that the recurrence is not governed by a small modular period like the regular tetrahedron factor. Compare the elementary factor z^2 - z + 1: it divides P_{a,b} exactly when 6 divides a+b, producing many rows. The -23 cubics do not behave that way in the scanned range. They appear as isolated events: one direct pin and one hidden cofactor.

The hidden cofactor is especially important. At (5,1), P_{5,1} factors as the regular tetrahedron factor z^2 - z + 1 times z^3 - 4z^2 + 5z - 1. The first factor is cyclotomic, discriminant -3, a familiar abelian/torsion tetrahedral piece. The second factor is the 5_2 core field. Thus a corner that looks elementary from its visible cyclotomic component quietly carries the non-abelian core as a cofactor. This is a miniature version of the entire Shadow-Core Lift: an abelian factor is visible, but the real non-abelian field is the hidden complementary factor. The corner family therefore becomes a laboratory for shadow-core factorization.

The natural computational next step is to build a corner factor atlas in which every factor receives a shadow/core type. For each P_{a,b}, factor over Q, compute discriminants, signatures, Galois markers when feasible, Bloch values at complex roots, and possible census-volume matches. Then classify factors as elementary cyclotomic, abelian non-cyclotomic, non-abelian cubic, non-abelian higher degree, or unresolved. The scan through a<=20 already recovers the expected small fields: z^2 - 3z + 1 with discriminant 5 at (2,1), the -31 cubic at (3,1), the -23 cubic at (3,2), the -283 quartics at (4,1) and (4,3), the split -3 and -23 at (5,1), and the quintic rows with discriminants 3017, 3233, 2869, plus the (7,5) split row with the -3 factor and quintic discriminant 1649. The numbers themselves are less important than the classification behavior: some rows are direct core pins, some are elementary shadows, and some are higher-degree candidates rejected or unresolved by volume matching.

The breakthrough possibility is a quotient-ring search for hidden cores. Instead of scanning every P_{a,b} by full factorization, take a library of known core fields K_i with generator polynomials g_i and test divisibility by fast modular exponentiation. For each g_i, the condition g_i | P_{a,b} is simply (1-z)^a = z^b in the finite-dimensional algebra Q[z]/(g_i). This is a dynamical system on a finite-rank vector space over Q. It can be accelerated by representing multiplication by z and 1-z as matrices and searching for exponent pairs where A^a v = B^b v. Over reductions modulo good primes, the search becomes finite and very fast; rational confirmation follows only for hits. This is a more powerful engine than volume-first search because it can find hidden field factors even when no census volume is currently known.

For the -23 field, the scan suggests two distinguished relations in the field: (1-z)^3 = z^2 for one generator, and (1-z)^5 = z for the translated generator. These are not two unrelated coincidences. They are two ways the same field sits inside the corner dynamics, one as the direct 5_2 regulator corner and one as the AK geometric branch under a regular-tetrahedron split. The challenge is to identify the internal field-theoretic reason. Is there a unit equation in K_23 governing both? Are these relations conjugate under a cluster transformation z |-> 1-z, z |-> 1/z, or a Neumann-Zagier shape change? Does the pair (3,2) and (5,1) encode two triangulation presentations of the same Bloch class, one direct and one stabilized by a regular tetrahedron? These are not rhetorical questions; they are exact algebra questions.

A concrete next computation would enumerate unit equations (1-z)^a = z^b inside K_23 for all small-height generators z = r_0 + r_1 alpha + r_2 alpha^2 with bounded rational coefficients, not only for the four known polynomial avatars. The quotient-ring scan fixed g and varied (a,b). The inverse search fixes K_23 and searches for elements z in K_23 satisfying corner-like unit equations. This could reveal whether (3,2) and (5,1) are two points in a larger internal unit-equation orbit or truly isolated minimal solutions. If the only low-height solutions correspond to the known geometry, that itself is a theorem candidate: the 5_2 Bloch class is isolated among small corner unit equations in its own field. That would be a genuine beyond-core discovery.

VI. Plate E: p-adic ramification as a computational control surface

The p-adic side should not be treated as a separate annex. In a core-aware computing architecture, ramification is a control surface. The discriminant -23 says that 23 is the unique ramified rational prime of the cubic core field. The Habiro symmetrization line says denominators localize at 23: the relevant ring is Z[z,1/23]. The Condensoroid side says the mod-23 factorization has a double root and a recentered Newton polygon with slope behavior. The p-adic regulator side says that across the computed atlas rows a uniform depth appears. These are not independent facts. They all tell the computation where singular arithmetic lives.

A p-adic control surface has three jobs. First, it tracks denominators. If a perturbative coefficient in K_23 develops denominators supported at 23, the system should not mark that as arbitrary coefficient growth. It should recognize the ramified prime and attach the valuation to the field descriptor. Second, it selects branches. Hensel lifts near a double root behave differently from lifts near simple roots; the recentered Newton polygon tells the computation which local extension and which slope are present. Third, it helps separate genuine core behavior from accidental decimal agreement. A volume coincidence over C is less convincing if the p-adic skeleton and ramification behavior do not match. Conversely, a field whose complex regulator and p-adic branch both match has stronger custody.

For the Shadow-Core extractor, the p-adic stage should be placed after field recognition but before final regulator claims. Once an irreducible factor is found, compute its discriminant, ramified primes, factorization modulo ramified primes, Hensel data, and valuation behavior of the key denominators. For K_23, the mod-23 double-root phenomenon is not optional decoration. It is the finite-place shadow of the same field that produces the complex hyperbolic volume. The real regulator log rho, complex Bloch regulator D(z), and p-adic skeleton are three evaluations of one algebraic carrier. A computation that stores only the complex root has thrown away two-thirds of the architecture.

The p-adic control surface also connects to the coefficient-field virtual machine. Suppose c_n is a loop coefficient represented as a rational vector in 1,u,u^2. The CFVM can compute v_23 of its denominator and of its norm. Across coefficients, this produces a valuation profile. That profile may be as meaningful as the complex magnitude. If a hidden integrality law exists, it will likely appear as bounded denominator growth after a natural normalization, or as a predictable valuation slope tied to the loop order. The existing one-loop discriminant -23 * 7^2 already hints at mixed core/shadow arithmetic: ramified 23 from the core and determinant 7 from the shadow. Higher-loop coefficients may reveal whether this mixture follows a stable law.

A concrete computational program is therefore: compute c_0 through c_N as exact K_23 elements, normalize by expected powers of the Hessian and torsion factors, record denominator support, norm factorization, trace, and valuations at 23 and 7, and compare against the abelian Nahm shadow. If the non-abelian coefficients show a stable 23-adic depth that the Nahm shadow lacks, that becomes a new invariant separating full AK perturbation from q-hypergeometric shadow. If the 7-adic factors appear only through determinant-normalization terms while 23 controls ramified field denominators, then the phrase 7 is the shadow and -23 is the core becomes a coefficient-level theorem rather than a slogan.

The p-adic control surface also gives a better way to search beyond 5_2. For any candidate core field K, compute Ram(K), the set of ramified primes. Compare it to determinant primes, Alexander primes, cyclotomic conductor primes, and state-sum exceptional primes. A pure shadow case should have close agreement between determinant/conductor and field ramification. A non-abelian lift case may show a new ramified prime not predicted by the abelian shadow. For 5_2, determinant 7 and discriminant prime 23 are distinct. That split is a warning that determinant data is not enough. In other knots, a similar mismatch may predict where the shadow-core obstruction will appear.

This suggests a search heuristic: rank candidate objects by ramification surprise. Define Surprise(X)=Ram(CoreCandidate) minus primes visible in Shadow(X). If Surprise is empty, the object may still be interesting but is less likely to force a non-abelian lift. If Surprise contains a new prime with non-square discriminant and complex regulator support, prioritize it. For 5_2, Surprise includes 23 relative to determinant 7. For 4_1, the geometric field Q(sqrt(-3)) is cyclotomic and closely tied to the tetrahedral prime 3, so surprise is low. This does not prove anything, but it directs computation toward the cases where the abelian shadow is arithmetically incomplete.

The deepest version would combine p-adic and complex selection. A candidate core branch is accepted only if it passes an Archimedean test, D(z) nonzero and volume-compatible; a finite-place test, ramification and Hensel data coherent; and an algebraic test, irreducible factor/Galois marker not contained in the shadow field. This tri-test avoids chasing false positives. It also converts the word “custody” into a computational predicate: a branch has custody of an invariant when it carries the complex regulator, finite ramification, and exact coefficient field simultaneously. The -23 field passes this predicate for 5_2. The cyclic conductor-7 field passes a shadow predicate but fails the core predicate. That is the separation the next machine should automate.

VII. Plate F: the Cube-Root Spine as the obstruction-method cousin

The Cube-Root Spine should be included in the sequel, but not by forcing it into the discriminant--23 story. Its value is methodological. It proves that an apparent discriminant wall can be too crude, and that the true exactness locus may sit deeper inside a rank-defect stratum. In the sextic Weyl-exactness problem, the Moyal correction tower closes to two transvectant gates, (f,g)_3 and (f,g)_5. Along the cube-root line f_lambda = x^6 + lambda x^3y^3 + y^6, the exceptional value lambda=40/7 produces a finite exact spectrum. The theorem is not that an infinite channel opens; the theorem is that the exact corridor is real and finite. The reverse odd-degree gates then show that rank failure for squarefree septimic or nonic partners does not occur on the full discriminant wall but only on deep multiple-root strata such as a quintuple-root stratum.

This is precisely the same epistemic discipline as the Shadow-Core Lift. The visible wall is not the answer. In 5_2, determinant and cyclotomic conductor are visible walls; they are useful but too small. In the cube-root atlas, the ordinary discriminant is a visible wall; it is useful but too broad. In both settings the computation must push past the first algebraic surface and locate the true carrier. For 5_2 the carrier is the non-abelian character/AK branch. For the cube-root problem the carrier is a finite rank-defect locus in the transvectant gate map. The mathematical objects differ; the discovery pattern matches.

The practical lesson for computing is important. A generic solver tends to report walls: discriminants, singular matrices, zero determinants, bad primes, rank drops. But a discovery engine must refine walls into carriers. A determinant-zero Nahm matrix is a wall; the actual carrier of the -23 field is not that matrix saddle but the full AK critical ideal. A discriminant-zero binary form is a wall; the actual carrier of reverse-gate rank failure is a quintuple-root stratum, not an arbitrary repeated-root form. A cyclotomic root-of-unity degeneration is a wall; the actual non-abelian core requires a gluing/character lift. Thus the shared computational primitive is not discriminant detection. It is wall refinement.

The wall-refinement algorithm has a general form. Given a wall W defined by a coarse condition, decompose W into strata S_i. For each stratum compute the rank, field, obstruction class, or regulator behavior that actually matters. Then identify the minimal stratum S_* where the desired phenomenon occurs. In the cube-root case, W is the discriminant locus of the partner form, but S_* is the quintuple-root stratum for rank failure. In 5_2, W is the determinant-zero or cyclotomic degeneration boundary, but S_* is the full non-abelian AK/character branch. The same algorithm can be used in many domains: singular closures, deformation quantization exactness, state-integral critical loci, p-adic Newton polygon branches, and compiler-like elimination graphs.

This provides a better way to connect the cube-root program to the topological one without making a false field claim. The cube-root result is not evidence that -23 is universal. It is evidence that the method which found -23 can be generalized. The method says: when a visible obstruction appears, do not stop; stratify it. Compute the exact locus where the obstruction becomes productive. Distinguish empty walls from live corridors, degenerate branches from Morse branches, abelian shadows from non-abelian cores. The Cube-Root Spine is therefore the algebraic-exactness laboratory for the same extraction discipline that the 5_2 work demonstrates in quantum topology.

A computing breakthrough here would be a unified obstruction stratifier. It would accept an ideal, matrix family, transvectant gate, or critical equation. It would compute coarse walls by determinants/discriminants, then refine them by primary decomposition, rank profiles, multiplicity strata, Galois fields, and regulator tests. Its output would not be “singular” or “nonsingular”; it would be a stratified map of which singularities are productive. In the cube-root problem, productive means a Weyl-exact corridor. In the AK problem, productive means a non-abelian Morse saddle with a coefficient field. In p-adic skeletons, productive means a ramified branch with meaningful Newton slope. The same infrastructure could serve all these cases.

The philosophical gain is that it prevents overfitting to one beautiful number. The number -23 is extraordinary in the 5_2 program, but the larger discovery is the ability to extract hidden carriers from shadows. The cube-root atlas proves that this ability is not confined to hyperbolic knot fields. It works in invariant theory and deformation quantization too. The next document after this one could therefore be organized around “carrier extraction” rather than “disc -23.” The 5_2 field would be the first non-abelian carrier, the cube-root sextic would be the first finite Weyl-exact carrier, and future examples would be searched for by the same wall-refinement engine.

VIII. Beyond volume: regulator output is only the first observable

Hyperbolic volume is the most visible output because it is a single real number and it agrees spectacularly with Bloch-Wigner evaluation. But a core-aware program should not stop at volume. Volume is one regulator coordinate of the Bloch class. The same core field can control Chern-Simons values, one-loop torsion, higher-loop perturbative coefficients, p-adic regulators, and possibly exact recursions in q-holonomic or state-integral expansions. Treating volume as the final prize would repeat the error of treating determinant as the final shadow. The volume is a gateway observable.

For 5_2 the Bloch regulator line is already strong:

Vol(5_2)=3D(z), z in K_23, D(z)=Im Li_2(z)+arg(1-z)log|z|.

The Borel-Zagier line then relates zeta_K(2) to the same volume up to the explicit rational and pi/discriminant factors. The p-adic regulator line reads the same class at the ramified finite place. The perturbative line reads Hessian and vertex data at the same geometric saddle. These should be treated as components of a regulator vector rather than as separate facts. A future invariant could be written schematically as

Reg^sharp(X) = (Reg_infty^D(Bloch(X)), Reg_p(Bloch(X)), Torsion_1(X), Loop_2(X), ...),

with each coordinate computed in or from the same core field. The old scalar Theta is a shadow of this vector. The stronger object is the complete regulator-and-coefficient packet.

This shift suggests a new computational target: a regulator bundle. Given a core branch, compute all available regulator outputs from one shared field representation. At the complex embedding, evaluate Bloch-Wigner volume and, when branch choices are controlled, Chern-Simons phase. At the algebraic level, compute adjoint torsion and loop coefficients as field elements. At the p-adic level, compute Coleman/p-adic dilogarithm shadows or the available regulator depth. At the zeta level, compare Dedekind zeta values, L-functions, and Borel-Zagier ratios. The output is not a single number but a bundle whose internal consistency is itself evidence of correct branch selection.

For computing, the advantage is fault localization. If the volume matches but the field does not, the branch is suspicious. If the field matches but the p-adic ramification does not, the local place selection is suspicious. If one-loop coefficients leave the field, the perturbative derivation is suspicious. If the zeta ratio fails while volume and field match, the normalization or class multiple is suspicious. A regulator bundle lets the system determine which part of the lift has failed. This is much stronger than a global yes/no comparison.

The perturbative coefficient field gives a particularly promising next observable. The one-loop and two-loop values already sit in K_23. Higher loops should be computed exactly as rational functions of saddle variables and derivative tensors. If all c_n lie in K_23 after appropriate normalization, then the AK perturbative series is a K_23-valued transseries at the selected branch. If denominators follow a predictable support law, then the series may have a hidden arithmetic recurrence. If coefficient heights grow in a structured way, that structure may reflect the same conductor/shadow split. This is where “the core field is the coefficient field of quantum perturbation theory” becomes an experimental program rather than a sentence.

There is also a possible compression gain. A high-order perturbative series over complex numbers is expensive and brittle. A K-valued series is exact and structurally smaller. Suppose each coefficient c_n is stored as (a_n,b_n,c_n)/d_n in the basis 1,u,u^2. The dominant storage becomes rational height growth rather than decimal precision. If the denominator d_n factors only over a small prime set after normalization, the series is even more compressible. This is not generic data compression; it is semantic arithmetic compression. The field is the codec.

This connects unexpectedly to the user’s broader compression and compiler programs. A semantic codec succeeds when it identifies the latent grammar that generated data. Here the latent grammar of perturbative quantum data is not a byte pattern but a number field and a branch of a critical ideal. Storing decimals is like storing rendered pixels; storing field coordinates is like storing glyph IDs and layout instructions. The Shadow-Core Lift is therefore a mathematical version of semantic compression: recover the generating carrier and store data in that carrier. That analogy is not merely poetic. It suggests concrete code: a perturbative series format with a field header, embedding header, branch header, and exact coefficient vectors.

The future benchmark should compare three representations of the same loop expansion: raw complex decimals; high-precision algebraic recognition after the fact; and native field-valued computation from the beginning. The native method should win on exact equality, storage, reproducibility, branch control, and p-adic inspection. If it also wins on speed for high-order coefficients by avoiding repeated high-precision recognition, then the computational case becomes decisive. The breakthrough would be to show that core extraction is not only more truthful but more efficient.

IX. A core-aware search program for new examples

The natural next question is where to search after 5_2. The answer should not be “everywhere.” The Shadow-Core framework gives priorities. Search where the abelian shadow is strong enough to route the object but visibly insufficient to explain the candidate core. Search where determinant primes and trace-field discriminant primes diverge. Search where a cyclotomic or Nahm saddle exists but a geometric or character branch has larger Galois group. Search where a critical ideal splits into a degenerate abelian branch plus an irreducible positive-volume branch. Search where a corner-divisor equation contains an elementary cyclotomic factor and a non-abelian cofactor. Search where p-adic ramification introduces a prime not seen by the determinant.

The first domain is small hyperbolic knots and cusped census manifolds with cubic (1,1) trace fields. The existing atlas already found 28 rows across nine fields with rational Theta values in powers of 2 and 3. That atlas should now be reinterpreted through shadow-core extraction. For each row, compute determinant or first-homology shadow, trace-field discriminant, Galois marker, Bloch volume, p-adic ramification depth, and available state-sum/Nahm data. Then ask which rows behave like 5_2: abelian shadow small, non-abelian core larger, perturbative coefficient field computable. The discriminant -31 and -283 pins should be treated as the next test cases. Do they have analogues of determinant-shadow split? Do their AK or character critical ideals factor into abelian and non-abelian branches? Do one-loop coefficients lie in their trace fields with mixed discriminant factors? These are concrete computations.

The second domain is twist knots. The 5_2 knot is a twist knot; 4_1 is the abelian/torsion calibration; 6_1 and 7_2 already have their own saddle and obstruction stories in the broader program. A twist-knot family may reveal how the shadow-core lift changes with crossing number and sign. For each twist knot, compute the colored Jones/Kashaev asymptotic saddle, trace field, determinant, Nahm candidate matrix if available, and AK critical ideal factorization. Classify whether the core field is cyclotomic, abelian non-cyclotomic, S_3 cubic, higher non-abelian, or obstructed. The goal is not to prove the volume conjecture for all twist knots; it is to classify where the abelian shadow stops.

The third domain is the corner-divisor family. It is computationally cheap and conceptually aligned. Use quotient-ring divisibility scans to search for known trace fields as exact factors. Then invert the search: for a desired field K, find low-height elements z in K satisfying (1-z)^a = z^b for small coprime a,b. This produces candidate Bloch classes and regulator values without requiring a manifold first. Some will match census manifolds; others may be pure arithmetic regulators. The distinction is useful. A field can have a regulator corner without being the invariant trace field of a known small manifold. The atlas should separate “field pin,” “volume near-hit,” “regulator-only class,” and “manifold custody.”

The fourth domain is q-hypergeometric/Nahm data. The 5_2 case showed that the Nahm-sum saddle may be abelian even when the full quantum field is non-abelian. That suggests searching for other cases where q-series presentations are shadows, not cores. For each Nahm matrix A, compute its saddle fields, kernel/free directions, conductor fields, and compare with known geometric trace fields. Cases where the fields disagree are not failures; they are lift candidates. The exact question is: which additional analytic structure must be added to move from the Nahm shadow to the geometric core? For 5_2 the answer is the full Andersen-Kashaev/Faddeev state integral. Other knots may require different completions.

The fifth domain is deformation quantization exactness, represented by the Cube-Root Spine. Search for Hamiltonians whose Weyl-exact partner spectra are finite but nontrivial, and classify the exactness carriers. Here the core is not a number field but a rank-defect module or invariant-theoretic stratum. Still, the same algorithms apply: compute coarse obstruction, refine wall, identify minimal productive stratum, store exact carrier. A cross-domain database could then compare carrier types: trace field, Bloch class, p-adic skeleton, transvectant kernel, finite conductor module, or semantic compression grammar.

A core-aware search program should return negative results with the same care as positives. The corner scan through a<=300 did not find additional divisibility hits for the two -23 cubic avatars. That negative result is useful because it argues against a simple periodic law. The rapidity degeneration obstruction is a stronger negative result because it proves cyclotomic routes cannot reach S_3. The Nahm-sum separation is another negative result because it proves the det-zero matrix saddle is abelian. In the new search, a failed containment or absent field should be stored as evidence. Breakthroughs often occur when the system can say exactly where something is not.

The final output of the search program should be an atlas of lift types. Type 0: shadow complete, core abelian or absent. Type 1: abelian geometry, as with 4_1 and Q(sqrt(-3)). Type 2: non-abelian core with direct character/AK extraction, as with 5_2. Type 3: non-abelian core suspected by volume/regulator but missing branch custody. Type 4: shadow/core mismatch with unknown receiving space. Type 5: wall-refinement carrier outside topology, such as cube-root Weyl exactness. Such an atlas would be more valuable than another list of invariants because it tells a computation what kind of lift is required.

X. The computing architecture: CoreLift Engine v0

A concrete CoreLift Engine can be built in layers. The algebra layer contains polynomial arithmetic, quotient fields, resultants, discriminants, factorization, field isomorphism heuristics, norm/trace, and exact matrix operations. The numeric layer contains high-precision complex embeddings, Bloch-Wigner and related regulator evaluations, interval or ball arithmetic where possible, and branch-aware logarithm/dilogarithm lifting. The p-adic layer contains modular factorization, Hensel lifting, Newton polygon extraction, valuations, and denominator support. The topology/state-integral layer contains gluing equation ingestion, Neumann-Zagier matrices, AK/Faddeev potential templates, A-polynomial/resultant elimination, and branch typing. The search layer contains corner scans, quotient-ring divisibility tests, determinant-shadow comparison, and Galois-containment obstruction tests.

The key design decision is that every layer exchanges typed carriers, not untyped numbers. A field object is not just a polynomial; it includes reduced polynomial, discriminant, signature, embeddings, ramified primes, isomorphism maps, and known aliases. A branch object is not just a root; it includes exact factor, multiplicity, field, volume status, Hessian status, and shadow/core classification. A coefficient object is not just a decimal; it includes field vector, denominator, embedding, norm, trace, and valuation profile. A regulator object is not just a real number; it includes Bloch class expression, embedding, branch choices, numerical value, and precision certificate. This typed design is what prevents the old failure mode: a solver finds a decimal, recognizes a polynomial, and then silently forgets which branch generated it.

The minimal v0 should implement five commands. The command shadow object computes determinant-level and abelian data from available input. The command obstruct shadow target runs Galois, cyclotomic, discriminant, and rank/kernel obstruction tests. The command eliminate-critical input computes exact critical ideal factors for a gluing or AK template. The command branch-type factors evaluates factor degree, discriminant, Galois marker, Bloch volume if applicable, and Hessian nondegeneracy when derivative data are present. The command coeff-field branch expressions reduces symbolic loop expressions into the branch quotient field and emits exact coefficient vectors plus minimal polynomials. With only these five commands, the 5_2 story can be reproduced as computation rather than prose.

The first benchmark should be the 5_2 known path. Input: determinant 7, Habiro/Nahm matrix A, m015 gluing equations or embedded critical ideal template, and loop expressions. Expected output: shadow determinant 7; Nahm saddle fields Q(sqrt2) and Q(zeta_7)^+; obstruction that the S_3 -23 field is not cyclotomic and not in the Nahm saddle; critical factorization (z-1)^2 times z^3 - 4z^2 + 5z - 1; branch typing parabolic versus hyperbolic; one-loop and two-loop coefficient minimal polynomials; Bloch-Wigner regulator value. The benchmark passes only if all facts are produced as typed objects and if the system refuses to label the Nahm saddle as the non-abelian core.

The second benchmark should be 4_1 as a calibration. Input: figure-eight gluing data. Expected output: field Q(sqrt(-3)), discriminant -3, cyclotomic containment in Q(zeta_3), positive Bloch volume, torsion/abelian compatibility. The point is to prove the engine does not force every hyperbolic object into Type 2 non-abelian lift. Some cores are already contained in abelian/cyclotomic closures. 4_1 is the control that keeps the theory honest.

The third benchmark should be the corner family. Input: P_{a,b} for a<=N and a library of fields. Expected output: exact factor table, elementary z^2-z+1 rule, direct -23 hit at (3,2), hidden -23 cofactor at (5,1), no additional exact divisibility hits for the two tested -23 avatars through a declared bound, and field classifications for -31 and -283 pins. This benchmark tests whether the engine can discover rather than merely verify known gluing equations.

The fourth benchmark should be Cube-Root Spine. Input: the transvectant gate maps for the sextic exactness problem. Expected output: coarse discriminant wall, refined rank-defect stratum, finite Weyl-exact spectrum, and negative squarefree odd gaps. This benchmark tests whether the architecture is genuinely about carrier extraction rather than only about knot trace fields. The engine must handle a carrier that is a rank stratum instead of a number field.

From a software standpoint, the CoreLift Engine should be small and exact-first. Sympy/Sage/PARI can handle polynomial and number-field arithmetic; mpmath or Arb-style ball arithmetic can handle regulator numerics; SnapPy or pre-exported gluing rows can feed topology; custom quotient-ring scans can handle corner searches quickly. The novelty is not any single backend. The novelty is the workflow contract: no numeric answer is final until it has been assigned to a typed carrier, and no lift is legal until a lower-level obstruction explains why the shadow was insufficient.

If this engine works, the computing breakthrough is broader than 5_2. It becomes a way to search for hidden structure in mathematical data sets: exact fields behind volumes, exact strata behind rank drops, exact grammars behind compression artifacts, exact p-adic branches behind denominators. The same architecture can serve quantum topology, deformation quantization, semantic codecs, and compiler optimization because it is built around a universal computational move: identify the projection, prove its insufficiency, extract the carrier, compute natively in the carrier.

XI. New conjectures suggested by the computation

The sequel should not hide its conjectural edge. The computations suggest several statements that are not yet proved but are precise enough to guide work. The first is the Isolated Corner Recurrence Conjecture for the 5_2 field. In the coprime corner family P_{a,b}=(1-z)^a - z^b, the two discriminant--23 cubic avatars z^3 - 2z^2 + 3z - 1 and z^3 - 4z^2 + 5z - 1 occur only at (3,2) and (5,1), respectively, up to the equivalences generated by the standard shape transformations z, 1-z, 1/z, 1/(1-z), z/(z-1), (z-1)/z. The computation verified the raw divisibility form through a<=300 for two avatars, not the full shape-orbit conjecture. The next step is to formulate the shape-orbit action exactly and reduce the unit equation to a finite S-unit problem in K_23.

The second is the Coefficient-Field Persistence Conjecture. For the 5_2 Andersen-Kashaev expansion at the hyperbolic Morse branch, after a fixed normalization of the classical action and Hessian, every perturbative coefficient lies in the cubic trace field K_23. The current pass checks one-loop and two-loop inputs exactly. The conjecture says this persists to all orders because the potential derivatives and saddle coordinates are algebraic in the same quotient field, and the Feynman contractions use rational operations. The subtlety is not algebraic closure under rational operations; it is normalization, branch, and possible transcendental constants. The conjecture should be stated with those normalizations explicit.

The third is the Mixed Prime Law. In normalized perturbative invariants for 5_2, the prime 23 enters through core-field ramification and the prime 7 enters through determinant/shadow torsion factors. The one-loop discriminant -23 * 7^2 is the first clue. The law would predict that higher-loop minimal polynomials or denominator norms factor into a stable pattern separating ramified core primes from determinant-shadow primes and universal small primes. This is computationally testable once c_2, c_3, and beyond are computed exactly.

The fourth is the Branch Split Principle. When a shadow-core lift is complete for a hyperbolic knot with a non-abelian trace field, the relevant analytic critical ideal should contain, after appropriate elimination, at least one degenerate abelian boundary component and at least one irreducible non-abelian Morse component. The 5_2 factorization (z-1)^2 times the -23 cubic is the prototype. The principle may fail in this simple form for more complicated manifolds, but if a generalized version holds, it gives a powerful search heuristic: look for factorizations that internally display the split between shadow and core.

The fifth is the Wall Refinement Principle. In both the topological and cube-root programs, the first visible wall is too coarse. The determinant-zero matrix does not carry the non-abelian field; the full AK critical branch does. The ordinary discriminant wall does not characterize reverse Weyl-exact rank failure; the quintuple-root stratum does. The conjecture is that productive mathematical obstructions almost always live on refined strata of coarse singular walls, and a discovery engine should privilege stratification over scalar scoring. This is broad, but it is computationally meaningful: algorithms should output stratified carriers rather than global wall flags.

The sixth is the Semantic Field Compression Principle. For exact perturbative or regulator data generated by a fixed non-abelian core, the natural compressed representation is the field plus coefficient vectors, not decimal approximations. If coefficient heights grow moderately after normalization, field-native storage will outperform high-precision decimal storage in reliability and possibly size. This is not a theorem yet; it is a benchmark proposal. Generate N perturbative coefficients, store them as decimals and as K-vectors, compare size, equality testing, regeneration cost, and branch safety. If the field codec wins, the Shadow-Core Lift becomes a computational compression principle.

The seventh is the Ramification Surprise Heuristic. Objects whose trace-field discriminants introduce primes absent from determinant/conductor shadows are high-priority candidates for non-abelian lifts. The 5_2 split between determinant 7 and discriminant prime 23 is the prototype. The heuristic should be tested across the 28-row cubic atlas and beyond. It is not sufficient: a new ramified prime does not guarantee a useful core. But it is a cheap ranking score for where the abelian shadow is likely incomplete.

These conjectures are deliberately mixed in strength. Some are near-theorems awaiting formalization; some are search heuristics; some are software benchmarks. That mixture is healthy. The program is not a single theorem but a computation-guided architecture. Its job is to turn observed structure into exact tests, exact tests into algorithms, algorithms into new data, and new data into sharper conjectures. The second pass therefore should not pretend that everything is settled. It should identify the frontier sharply enough that the next computation can fail usefully.

XIII. Dense mathematical closure: the lift as a functorial refusal

The cleanest formal sentence is that the Shadow-Core Lift is a functorial refusal of premature abelianization. There is a projection pi from the full geometric or analytic object to its abelian shadow. For many computations pi is useful and perhaps optimal. But for a non-abelian core object, pi is not faithful. It identifies or erases data required for regulator and perturbative computation. The lift is not the inverse of pi; generally no inverse exists. It is a legal enlargement chosen after proving that pi has lost the required invariant. This is why obstruction is central. Without obstruction, the lift is arbitrary complexity. With obstruction, the lift is forced.

In 5_2 the projection pi sends the full object to determinant 7, cyclotomic coloring data, Gauss sums, and an abelian Nahm saddle. The invariant lost by pi is the S_3 trace field K_23. The obstruction is Galois-theoretic and saddle-theoretic: K_23 cannot sit in a cyclotomic field, and it is not in the Q(sqrt2) times Q(zeta_7)^+ Nahm saddle. The receiving object is the AK/character critical ideal. The exact factorization proves that the receiving object contains both the abelian boundary branch and the non-abelian core branch. Therefore the lift is not a vague instruction to use more geometry. It is a precise map from denied containment to exact branch extraction.

The coefficient-field claim adds another layer. If the core were only a trace-field label, the lift would be classificatory. But the one-loop and two-loop calculations show that perturbative quantities are rational functions in the core coordinate. Hence the lift changes the arithmetic of computation. In the shadow world, coefficients appear as unexplained complex numbers or abelian approximations. In the core world, they are exact elements of K_23. The field is the native coordinate ring for the expansion. The numerical embedding is a representation of that exact object, not the object itself.

This has philosophical consequences but also software consequences. A mathematical program that stores only the embedded complex numbers has already applied pi again: it has projected the exact algebraic coefficient into a decimal shadow. That may be acceptable for plotting, but it is not acceptable for identity, comparison, p-adic inspection, or long-range computation. The right software object is therefore not Complex but AlgebraicCoefficient(FieldID, vector, embedding). The right branch object is not Root but CriticalBranch(factor, field, type, embedding, regulator, Hessian). The right invariant object is not Volume but RegulatorBundle. The Shadow-Core Lift thus pushes all the way down into data structures.

This is why the second pass can honestly claim to seek computing breakthroughs. The breakthrough is not a faster approximation to a known number. It is a different representation of the problem: exact carrier first, numerical value second. The same shift appears in semantic compression, where storing the generative structure beats storing pixels; in compiler optimization, where preserving high-level blocks beats flattening early; in deformation quantization, where exact transvectant kernels beat global impossible quantization; and in quantum topology, where the trace field and critical branch beat determinant shadows. The shared law is: do not flatten before the obstruction has been classified.

The final mathematical object may be written as a tower:

Shadow_0(X) <= Shadow_1(X) <= Presentation(X) <= Critical(X) <= Core(X) <= Output(X).

The arrows are not always inclusions; they may be projections, degenerations, or dispatches. But the tower records the increasing ability to compute invariants. Shadow_0 might be determinant. Shadow_1 might be cyclotomic conductor. Presentation might be Nahm or q-series data. Critical might be the AK/character ideal. Core is the selected non-abelian branch. Output is regulator and perturbative coefficient data. The task of the engine is to know where each invariant first becomes visible. For 5_2, determinant appears at Shadow_0, conductor 7 at Shadow_1/Nahm, but the -23 field appears at Critical/Core, and loop coefficients appear at Core/Output.

This “first visibility” concept is useful. Define vis(I,X) as the lowest layer of the tower where invariant I is computable without loss. Then vis(det)=Shadow, vis(K_23)=Critical, vis(volume)=Core regulator, vis(one-loop)=Core coefficient field, vis(Stokes dominance)=full contour homology. A mature atlas would list invariants by first visibility. This prevents one of the most common errors in mathematical synthesis: seeing an invariant at a high layer and retroactively attributing it to a lower layer. The 5_2 work specifically corrected that error for the Nahm free direction. The -23 field was expected there; the computation showed it was not there. Its first visibility is the full AK/character critical layer.

The sequel therefore ends with a sharper doctrine. The point is not to unify everything by saying all layers are the same. They are not. The point is to build a machine that knows which layer carries which invariant, proves when a lower layer cannot carry it, and computes natively in the first layer where it appears. That is the Shadow-Core Lift as a computational theory.

XV. Experimental protocols for the first breakthrough computation

The sequel becomes most useful if it ends not with aspiration but with an experimental protocol. The first protocol is the coefficient persistence protocol. Input the AK branch polynomial u^3 - 4u^2 + 5u - 1, the symbolic potential derivatives in the chosen normalization, and the known rational expressions for one-loop and two-loop data. The system constructs K_23, reduces all derivative tensors into K_23, computes Gaussian contractions algebraically, and emits c_0, c_1, c_2, ... as vectors in the basis 1,u,u^2. At each order it records minimal polynomial, norm, trace, denominator factorization, v_23, v_7, and complex embedding under sigma_C^+. The pass condition is not that the decimals look plausible. The pass condition is that the coefficients reduce exactly in K_23 and that the denominator/valuation profile is reproducible. The first failure condition is a coefficient that leaves K_23 after all normalizations are included. The second failure condition is uncontrolled branch dependence. Either failure is informative: it identifies the missing analytic normalization or the point where the coefficient-field statement must be weakened.

The second protocol is the hidden corner protocol. Begin with the known K_23 field, generate a library of low-height elements z = a0 + a1 alpha + a2 alpha^2 with bounded rational height, and test the unit equation (1-z)^a = z^b for coprime a>b. For every solution compute the minimal polynomial of z, discriminant, shape-transform orbit, Bloch-Wigner value under complex embeddings, and whether P_{a,b} has elementary cyclotomic factors. This inverts the earlier scan. Instead of asking whether a fixed polynomial divides many corners, it asks which field elements of K_23 generate corner relations. If the only low-height solutions are the visible (3,2) and hidden (5,1) relations up to shape transformations, one obtains strong evidence for isolation. If new relations appear, they become candidates for new regulator identities. The important point is that the search is exact over K_23 until the final regulator evaluation.

The third protocol is the branch inventory protocol. For each candidate knot or census manifold, ingest gluing equations or precomputed Neumann-Zagier rows and eliminate to low-dimensional critical factors. For each factor compute degree, discriminant, square/non-square status, field signature, volume test, Hessian test, and relation to determinant/conductor shadow. The output is a branch table. The program should explicitly label parabolic, cyclotomic, abelian hyperbolic, non-abelian hyperbolic, and unresolved branches. A new discovery occurs when the table contains an abelian boundary branch and a non-abelian Morse branch whose field is not visible in the determinant/cyclotomic/Nahm shadow. That is the 5_2 pattern generalized. A negative discovery occurs when every geometric branch is already cyclotomic or abelian; that identifies a shadow-complete case.

The fourth protocol is the ramification surprise protocol. For a corpus of manifolds, knots, or corner equations, compute shadow primes from determinant, conductor, and obvious cyclotomic data, then compute core primes from trace-field discriminants and denominator norms. Rank objects by the set difference CorePrimes minus ShadowPrimes. For high-surprise objects, prioritize full branch extraction and coefficient-field computation. For low-surprise objects, use them as calibrations. This protocol is cheap and should be run before expensive state-integral numerics. It is not a theorem, but it is a search heuristic grounded in the 5_2 split between 7 and 23.

The fifth protocol is the wall-refinement protocol. Given a coarse singular wall such as determinant zero, discriminant zero, rank drop, or bad-prime set, decompose it into refined strata and test which stratum actually carries the desired invariant. In the cube-root problem, this means separating the whole discriminant wall from the quintuple-root rank-failure stratum. In the AK problem, this means separating the determinant-zero Nahm presentation from the full non-abelian critical branch. In p-adic computations, this means separating simple-root Hensel branches from recentered double-root ramification. The pass condition is a minimal productive stratum, not merely detection of singularity.

The sixth protocol is the field-codec benchmark. Take an exact perturbative series computed through N terms and serialize it three ways: decimal complex numbers at a fixed precision, algebraic numbers recognized after numerical computation, and native field vectors computed directly in K. Compare file size, equality testing, branch reproducibility, re-embedding cost, and p-adic inspectability. The hypothesis is that native field vectors win in reliability immediately and may win in storage once N is large or precision is high. This benchmark matters because it converts an abstract mathematical insight into a computing claim. If the field is truly the coefficient carrier, then using it as the storage grammar should outperform using its rendered decimal shadow.

Together these protocols turn the sequel into an executable research plan. The first protocol tests whether the core field continues to control quantum perturbation. The second tests whether the corner appearances are isolated or part of a hidden unit-equation family. The third searches for new shadow-core lifts. The fourth prioritizes targets. The fifth generalizes the method beyond number fields. The sixth tests whether the whole architecture improves computation, not merely exposition. The immediate next file should therefore not be another monograph. It should be a small computational package: K_23 arithmetic, branch inventory, corner search, and coefficient-field expansion.
