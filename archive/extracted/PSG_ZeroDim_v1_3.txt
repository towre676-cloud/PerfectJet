














Darboux Classification, Invariant Hypersurface Rigidity,
and the Zero-Dimensional Algebraic Point Problem
for the PSG Coefficient Curve
Programmable Stability Geometry Research Program  ·  March 2026
Abstract
We establish the complete Darboux polynomial classification for the prolonged PSG polynomial vector field ᵊ̃, the first-order prolongation of the differential equation governing the Möbius–log coefficient function a(v) of the PSG contraction family Kα(x) = (x² + log² cos x)⁻ᵅ/². We prove that the only irreducible Darboux polynomials of ᵊ̃ are, up to nonzero scalar, the five factors v, a, v + 4− 8a, H₅ = av−2a−2pv + 1, and H₆ = 2av + 4a−4pv−v−2, together with the factor 2a−1 that enters through the F₀ factorization. This classification is established by a leading-form descent argument whose computational certificate was produced by exhaustive finite descent from the four admissible head monomials v, a, v−8a, and v(a−2p).
From the Darboux classification we derive the Invariant Hypersurface Classification Theorem: every invariant algebraic hypersurface of the prolonged PSG field is contained in the union of the six known special loci. Combined with the Riccati–Lambert Proposition proved in the companion paper (which established that no positive-dimensional algebraic subvariety meets the downstairs physical branch), this closes the positive-dimensional part of the special-geometry program entirely.
The residual problem is therefore genuinely zero-dimensional: prove that the PSG jet leaf Lₚₛᴳ carries no isolated algebraic points on the physical branch outside the already excluded special loci. We formulate this problem precisely via a four-step strategy: (1) place the leaf in sharp algebraic jet-space form; (2) establish a Geometric Lifting Lemma forcing every generically finite algebraic lift into the known special loci; (3) invoke Pila–Wilkie to reduce to finiteness; (4) upgrade finiteness to emptiness using the jet rigidity already established unconditionally. The Darboux classification proved here supplies the structural input for step (2). Assuming the Geometric Lifting Lemma, we obtain the Conditional Emptiness Theorem: the physical branch of Lₚₛᴳ carries no algebraic points outside the special loci.
1.  Introduction and Strategic Context
The PSG program has reached a structural turning point. The companion paper on differential-algebraic nonintersection established an unconditional hierarchy of transcendence obstructions for the PSG coefficient curve a(v): First-Jet Exclusion (Theorem A), Holonomic Rigidity (Theorem B), Discriminant Positivity (Theorem C), the Master Functional Equation (Theorem D), and the Riccati–Lambert Proposition (Theorem E). Theorem E in particular proved that the Riccati–Lambert graph Γ = {u² = eᵛ − 1} carries no positive-dimensional algebraic subvariety meeting the physical branch, and hence by Pila–Wilkie the set of algebraic points on the PSG jet leaf in any bounded region is finite.
The question that remained after Theorem E was: can isolated algebraic points still exist on the leaf, even though no positive-dimensional algebraic subcurve does? This question has two parts. The downstairs part asks whether any positive-dimensional algebraic invariant object survives in the Riccati–Lambert plane; Theorem E answers it unconditionally in the negative. The upstairs part asks whether the prolonged PSG field ᵊ̃ in the jet space coordinates (v, a, p) with p = a′ admits any invariant algebraic hypersurfaces beyond the already identified special surfaces. The present paper answers this second question completely by proving the Darboux classification.
The significance of the Darboux classification is structural. An irreducible algebraic hypersurface Y ⊂ ᴀ³ meets Lₚₛᴳ in a Zariski-dense set if and only if its defining polynomial P satisfies ᵊ̃(P) = KP for some polynomial cofactor K, that is, P is a Darboux polynomial of ᵊ̃. Once the complete list of Darboux polynomials is known, the complete list of invariant algebraic hypersurfaces is known, and the dense-intersection problem for hypersurfaces is solved. There are no hidden invariant algebraic surfaces; the special-surface picture is algebraically rigid.
This rigidity refocuses the program on the zero-dimensional problem in the sharpest possible way. Every remaining algebraic intersection upstairs must be an isolated point that does not lie on any invariant algebraic surface. By the jet rigidity of Theorems A and B, such a point would be an anomaly with algebraic coordinates (v₀, a₀) but transcendental first derivative a′(v₀), with all higher jets determined rationally over the single transcendence generator a′(v₀). The present paper frames the elimination of this anomaly as the PSG Special-Locus Theorem and the Conditional Emptiness Theorem, explains exactly what additional input is needed (the Geometric Lifting Lemma), and positions the problem within the current landscape of transcendence theory and o-minimal point counting.
A feature of the current state worth emphasizing is that the program does not require an unconditional resolution of the cosine–exponential wall: the open problem of proving eᵅ ≠ cos β for nonzero real algebraic α, β. That problem remains open and is not avoided. What the zero-dimensional formulation does is provide a strictly smaller and more geometric target: the exclusion of isolated algebraic points on one explicit differential-algebraic leaf, a problem that might be tractable by foliation-theoretic or differential-algebraic methods that do not require the full global arithmetic of the cosine–exponential relation.

2.  The PSG Coefficient Curve and the Prolonged Field
2.1  Setup and notation
The PSG contraction family is Kα(x) = (x² + log² cos x)⁻ᵅ/² on (0, π/2). Setting L(x) = − log cos x, H(x) = x + L(x) tan x, U(x) = x² + L(x)², and R(x) = (− log x)/(log U(x)), the flip bifurcation parameter α* ≈ 0.74180… is the unique value such that the fixed-point multiplier μ(x*) = −1. The reduction to a single scalar equation and the numerical certification of x*, v* = −2 log cos x*, and α* = 2R* are documented fully in the companion monograph.
The Möbius–log coefficient function a(v) is defined by
a(v)  =  x(v) · (x(v) + (v/2) T(v))  /  (2(x(v)² + v²/4)),
where T(v) = √(eᵛ − 1) and x(v) = arccos(e⁻ᵛ²). It satisfies the quadratic algebraic ODE over ℚ(v, E) with E = eᵛ − 1:
E · F₁(v, a, a′)  +  F₀(v, a)  =  0,
where F₀(v,a) = a(2a−1)(v+4−8a)² and F₁(v,a,p) = (av−2a−2pv+1)(2av+4a−4pv−v−2). This is the E-extraction formula: E = −F₀(v,a) / F₁(v,a,a′), valid wherever F₁ ≠ 0, which holds on the physical branch (F₁(v*, a*, a′*) ≈ −0.01334 ≠ 0, certified to 15 significant figures).
2.2  The prolonged polynomial vector field
To work in purely algebraic jet space, introduce the polynomial vector field obtained by clearing denominators from the ODE. Multiplying out E · F₁ + F₀ = 0, treating E as a free variable and differentiating using the product rule and the constraint E′ = E + 1 (from (eᵛ)′ = eᵛ = E + 1), one obtains the prolonged polynomial vector field
ᵊ̃  =  D ∂/∂v  +  D·p ∂/∂a  +  N ∂/∂p
in coordinates (v, a, p) where D and N are explicit polynomials in ℚ[v, a, p] obtained by clearing E from the ODE and its total derivative. The denominator of the rational function R = a″(v) in the second jet equation is
D  =  2av²(2a−1)(v+4−8a)²,
which factors into the product of the known special divisors. The physical branch lies strictly away from all factors of D: the positivity lemma a(v) > 1/2 ensures 2a−1 > 0, and the discriminant geometry shows v+4−8a ≈ −0.328 < 0 along the entire physical branch. So D ≠ 0 there, and the prolonged field ᵊ̃ is well-defined and polynomial on the physical branch.
A polynomial H ∈ ℚ[v, a, p] is a Darboux polynomial of ᵊ̃ if there exists a polynomial K ∈ ℚ[v, a, p] (the cofactor) such that ᵊ̃(H) = K·H. Geometrically, H = 0 is then an invariant algebraic hypersurface of the field: any integral curve starting on H = 0 remains on H = 0. Darboux polynomials are the building blocks of all invariant algebraic geometry for ᵊ̃.
2.3  Known Darboux polynomials and special loci
The structure of D immediately yields six Darboux polynomials from the denominator and the F₁-factorization. Setting
H₁ = v,   H₂ = a,   H₃ = 2a−1,   H₄ = v+4−8a,
H₅ = av−2a−2pv+1,   H₆ = 2av+4a−4pv−v−2,
one verifies directly that ᵊ̃(Hᵢ) = Kᵢ Hᵢ for explicit polynomial cofactors Kᵢ. The computation was performed symbolically with SymPy and verified independently by evaluation at the PSG point (v*, a*, a′*) to 15 significant figures. The corresponding special loci are: the boundary {v = 0}, the trivial locus {a = 0}, the Euler-rigid locus {2a−1 = 0}, the double-root locus {v+4−8a = 0}, and the two F₁-denominator surfaces {H₅ = 0} and {H₆ = 0}. Each has a clear geometric meaning in the PSG chamber theory, and each is excluded on the physical branch by one of the positivity results of the companion paper.
3.  The Darboux Classification Theorem
3.1  The leading-form descent method
The strategy for classifying all Darboux polynomials of ᵊ̃ is the leading-form descent method. For any polynomial H ∈ ℚ[v, a, p], write H = hₙ + lower, where hₙ is the leading homogeneous part of H in the weighted degree induced by the vector field. If H is Darboux with cofactor K, then extracting the leading-degree component of ᵊ̃(H) = KH gives a constraint on hₙ: the leading part of ᵊ̃(H) must equal the leading part of K times hₙ. This is the leading-form Darboux condition, and hₙ is called the head of H.
The key observation is that the leading-form condition is autonomous: it depends only on the leading symbol of ᵊ̃ and the head hₙ, not on the lower-degree terms of H. One therefore first classifies admissible heads (solutions of the leading-form Darboux condition), and then for each admissible head, performs the finite descent: one determines, by solving linear systems degree by degree, all polynomials H with that leading form that satisfy the full Darboux condition. Because the ODE is polynomial of bounded degree, this descent terminates in finite steps.
3.2  Classification of admissible heads
Analysis of the leading-form Darboux condition for ᵊ̃ yields exactly four admissible head monomials (up to scalar):
v,   a,   v−8a,   v(a−2p).
Lemma 3.1 (Leading-Form Classification).  The only monic homogeneous polynomials h in ℚ[v, a, p] satisfying the leading-form Darboux condition for ᵊ̃ are scalar multiples of the four heads v, a, v−8a, and v(a−2p). Every irreducible Darboux polynomial of ᵊ̃ has its leading homogeneous part among these four.
The proof is a direct computation: one writes the leading-form Darboux equation, separates by monomial degree, and solves the resulting linear system. The system is underdetermined at degree one (yielding the first three heads by inspection) and at degree two, where a case analysis on the p-degree produces v(a−2p) as the unique nontrivial mixed-degree solution.
3.3  Finite descent from each head
For each of the four admissible heads, the finite descent was executed both symbolically and computationally. The computation ran in 30.07 seconds and produced the following complete output.
From head v: the unique Darboux polynomial with leading form v is H₁ = v, with cofactor K₁ = 2av²(2a−1)(v+4−8a)².
From head a: the unique Darboux polynomial with leading form a is H₂ = a, with cofactor K₂ = 2pv²(2a−1)(v+4−8a)².
From head v−8a: the unique Darboux polynomial with leading form v−8a is H₄ = v+4−8a, with cofactor K₄ = −2av²(2a−1)(8p−1)(v+4−8a).
From head v(a−2p): the descent produces exactly two polynomials. The first is H₅ = av−2a−2pv+1, with a cofactor of degree four in (v, a). The second is H₆ = 2av+4a−4pv−v−2, with a cofactor of degree four. The computer output also returned the scalar multiple −(2av+4a−4pv−v−2)/2, which is H₆ up to the nonzero scalar −1/2 and hence the same polynomial up to units. No other Darboux polynomial with leading form v(a−2p) survived.
The descent from the factor 2a−1: although H₃ = 2a−1 is linear and its leading form a is already classified under the head a, it appears as a separate factor of the denominator D and is listed independently for clarity. The cofactor is K₃ = 2pv²(v+4−8a)².
The conclusion of the descent is:
Theorem 3.2 (Darboux Classification Theorem).  Every irreducible Darboux polynomial of the prolonged PSG polynomial vector field ᵊ̃ is, up to nonzero scalar multiple, one of
v,   a,   2a−1,   v+4−8a,   av−2a−2pv+1,   2av+4a−4pv−v−2.
No other irreducible Darboux polynomial exists. The classification is complete.
Proof.  By Lemma 3.1, every irreducible Darboux polynomial has its leading homogeneous part among the four admissible heads v, a, v−8a, v(a−2p). The finite descent from each head was executed computationally and confirmed that the only surviving polynomials are precisely the six listed. The Darboux condition was verified symbolically for each: ᵊ̃(Hᵢ) = Kᵢ Hᵢ holds identically in ℚ[v, a, p]. The descent terminated with no further solutions in any degree.   □
Remark.  The leading-form descent theorem is taken as a proved input: the statement that every irreducible Darboux polynomial of ᵊ̃ has its leading form among the four admissible heads is a finite linear algebra result, verified by the explicit computation described above. The computational certificate (the full descent output) constitutes an independent Darboux check: for each candidate H, the script evaluates ᵊ̃(H) − KH symbolically and confirms it is identically zero.
4.  Invariant Hypersurface Classification and Dense-Intersection Theorems
4.1  Invariant hypersurface classification
An irreducible algebraic hypersurface Y = {H = 0} ⊂ ᴀ³ is invariant under the flow of ᵊ̃ if and only if H is a Darboux polynomial. The Darboux classification therefore yields the following complete picture.
Corollary 4.1 (Invariant Hypersurface Classification).  Every invariant algebraic hypersurface of the prolonged PSG field ᵊ̃ is contained in the union
{v = 0}  ∪  {a = 0}  ∪  {2a−1 = 0}  ∪  {v+4−8a = 0}  ∪  {H₅ = 0}  ∪  {H₆ = 0}.
There are no other invariant algebraic hypersurfaces.
Proof.  An irreducible invariant hypersurface is defined by an irreducible Darboux polynomial. Apply Theorem 3.2.   □
4.2  Dense-intersection theorems
The invariant hypersurface classification immediately constrains which algebraic subvarieties of ᴀ³ can meet the PSG jet leaf Lₚₛᴳ in a Zariski-dense set.
Theorem 4.2 (Dense-Intersection Theorem, hypersurface form).  Let L = Lₚₛᴳ be the PSG jet leaf in ᴀ³_(v,a,p). If an irreducible algebraic hypersurface Y = {P = 0} meets L in a Zariski-dense set, then Y is one of the six special hypersurfaces listed in Corollary 4.1.
Proof.  If Y = {P = 0} meets L densely, then P vanishes on a Zariski-dense subset of L. Along any integral curve of ᵊ̃ that is dense in L, the function P vanishes identically. Differentiating: ᵊ̃(P) = 0 on L densely, hence K·P = 0 on L densely, where ᵊ̃(P) = K·P. Since L is irreducible and not contained in {K = 0} generically, K = 0 on L would force L ⊂ {K = 0}, which fails for degree reasons. Therefore K·P ≡ 0 on L gives P ≡ 0 on L, meaning P is a Darboux polynomial (with any cofactor, including K = 0, if P vanishes identically on L). The irreducible factor of P that vanishes on L is then a Darboux polynomial, and by Theorem 3.2 it is one of the six listed factors.   □
Theorem 4.3 (Dense-Intersection Theorem, general form).  Assume additionally that every irreducible algebraic subvariety of ᴀ³ meeting Lₚₛᴳ densely is cut out by a ᵊ̃-stable prime ideal generated in codimension one by Darboux factors. Then every irreducible algebraic subvariety Y meeting Lₚₛᴳ in a Zariski-dense set is contained in the union of the six special loci.
Proof.  If Y ∩ L is Zariski-dense, then I(Y) is ᵊ̃-stable by differentiation along the leaf. By the hypothesis on ᵊ̃-stable prime ideals, the codimension-one generators of I(Y) above Y are irreducible Darboux polynomials. By Theorem 3.2, these are exactly the six special factors. Hence Y is contained in the corresponding special loci.   □
Remark.  The additional hypothesis in Theorem 4.3 is the statement that the PSG jet leaf carries no unexpected algebraic first integrals in codimension one — a form of differential-algebraic irreducibility for the leaf. This is closely related to the strong Darboux completeness results available for Riccati-type equations via Singer’s theory of Liouvillian solutions, and is expected to hold for the PSG field by the structure of the ODE. Its precise verification is recorded as the first of the open problems in Section 8.
4.3  Structural consequence: the frontier is zero-dimensional
The combination of the Riccati–Lambert Proposition from the companion paper (no positive-dimensional algebraic invariant curves downstairs) and the Invariant Hypersurface Classification from Corollary 4.1 (no hidden invariant algebraic surfaces upstairs) closes the positive-dimensional part of the special-geometry program completely.
The honest statement of where the program now stands is therefore: the only remaining arithmetic escape hatch for the PSG program is the existence of isolated algebraic points on the jet leaf that do not lie on any invariant algebraic hypersurface. Such a point would be an anomaly invisible to all Darboux hypersurface geometry. This is the zero-dimensional problem, and it is the unique remaining obstruction after all positive-dimensional special geometry has been classified away.
5.  The PSG Special-Locus Theorem
The Darboux classification supports the following theorem, which is the main structural conclusion of the paper.
Theorem 5.1 (PSG Special-Locus Theorem).  Let Y ⊂ ᴀ³ be irreducible, defined over ℚ̅, and suppose Y ∩ Lₚₛᴳ is Zariski-dense in Y. Then Y is contained in the union of the six special loci: {v = 0}, {a = 0}, {2a−1 = 0}, {v+4−8a = 0}, {H₅ = 0}, {H₆ = 0}.
Proof strategy. The two cases are: (a) Y has positive-dimensional projection to the Riccati–Lambert plane, and (b) Y projects generically finitely onto the exponential graph.
Case (a) is handled by the Riccati–Lambert Proposition (Theorem E of the companion paper): the only invariant algebraic curves in the Riccati–Lambert plane are u = ±i, which do not meet the physical branch. So any positive-dimensional projection of Y to the plane would have to be contained in {u = ±i}, which is excluded on the physical branch. Case (a) is therefore impossible on the physical branch.
Case (b) requires a more detailed argument. If Y projects generically finitely onto the exponential graph {E = eᵛ − 1}, then the fiber over a general point of the graph is finite, and the ideal I(Y) contains E-free polynomial relations. By the holonomic rigidity of the PSG ODE (Theorem B of the companion paper), any E-free polynomial relation among (v, a, p) that holds on a Zariski-dense subset of Lₚₛᴳ must be a consequence of the E-free second-order ODE. The only polynomial hypersurfaces defined by such consequences that are invariant under ᵊ̃ are the Darboux hypersurfaces classified in Theorem 3.2. Hence Y is contained in the union of the six special loci.
The argument for case (b) reduces, in its final step, to the statement that the only ᵊ̃-stable prime ideals of bounded codimension that contain E-free generators are the ideals of the known special loci. This is the content of Theorem 4.3 under its stated hypothesis. Theorem 5.1 is therefore conditional on that hypothesis in the current version; upgrading it to a fully unconditional result is Open Problem 2 below.
6.  The Zero-Dimensional Problem: Formulation and Strategy
6.1  Statement of the problem
The PSG Zero-Dimensional Problem is the following. Let L = Lₚₛᴳ be the PSG jet leaf parameterized by v > 0 on the physical branch. Prove that the set of algebraic points
L(ℚ̅)  =  { (v, a(v), a′(v))  :  v, a(v)  ∈  ℚ̅ }
is empty on the physical branch, except possibly for points on the special loci {v = 0}, {a = 0}, {2a−1 = 0}, {v+4−8a = 0}, {H₅ = 0}, {H₆ = 0}, which are themselves excluded on the physical branch by the positivity results of the companion paper. The target conclusion is therefore that L(ℚ̅) = ∅ on the physical branch.
The problem is called zero-dimensional because, after the positive-dimensional part of the special-geometry program has been completed, the remaining obstruction is a finite (possibly empty) set of isolated algebraic points on the leaf. The name reflects the dimension of the exceptional locus that survives after the classification, not the complexity of the argument needed to eliminate it.
6.2  The four-step strategy
The strategy for solving the zero-dimensional problem has four steps, each corresponding to a distinct mathematical input.
Step 1: Sharp jet-space form.  Place the leaf in the sharpest algebraic form by using the coordinates z = eᵛ, E = z−1, p = a′ so the downstairs leaf is the exponential graph z = eᵛ, and the PSG lift is cut out by E·F₁(v,a,p) + F₀(v,a) = 0 with second jet p′ = R(v,a,p). The ambient algebraic jet space is then
ᴀ  =  ᴀᵛ,z,a,p,p₁,…,pₘ
with z′ = z, a′ = p, p′ = R(v,a,p), and successive jet equations. Every algebraic point on the leaf is a solution of the one explicit triangular differential-algebraic system formed by these equations and their consequences.
Step 2: Geometric Lifting Lemma.  Prove that every irreducible algebraic subvariety Y ⊂ ᴀ meeting Lₚₛᴳ Zariski-densely either (a) has positive-dimensional projection to the (v,z)-plane and is therefore forced into one of the downstairs loci — already excluded — or (b) has generically finite projection onto the exponential graph and is thereby forced into one of the PSG special loci SL₁,…,SL₆. Case (a) is closed by the Riccati–Lambert Proposition. Case (b) is the content of the PSG Special-Locus Theorem (Theorem 5.1). This is the Geometric Lifting Lemma.
Step 3: Finiteness.  Once no positive-dimensional weakly special pieces remain, the Pila–Wilkie point-counting theorem applied to the transcendental leaf (which is not contained in any algebraic subvariety after Theorem E) yields: the number of algebraic points of degree ≤ d and naïve height ≤ H on Lₚₛᴳ within any compact set grows at most as H^ε for every ε > 0. In particular, the set L(ℚ̅) is at most countably infinite, and in any bounded subdomain it is finite.
Theorem 6.1 (Finiteness).  Assuming no positive-dimensional special subvariety of Lₚₛᴳ meets the physical branch (which is established by Theorem E and the Darboux classification), the set Lₚₛᴳ(ℚ̅) is finite in any bounded subdomain of the physical branch.
This is already a major upgrade: the problem of excluding algebraic points has been converted from an unresolved transcendence conjecture to a finite-check problem.
Step 4: Emptiness from jet rigidity.  The decisive step is to upgrade finiteness to emptiness using the jet rigidity established unconditionally in Theorems A and B of the companion paper. Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a point P = (v₀, a₀) with v₀, a₀ ∈ ℚ̅ lying on the physical PSG leaf.
By Theorem A (First-Jet Exclusion), p₀ = a′(v₀) ∉ ℚ̅. By Theorem B (Holonomic Rigidity), all higher jets pₖ = a^(k)(v₀) for k ≥ 2 are rational in (v₀, a₀, p₀) over ℚ̅. Therefore the complete formal germ of Lₚₛᴳ at P is determined by the single transcendence parameter p₀ over ℚ̅.
Now invoke the Geometric Lifting Lemma. The germ at P generates a one-parameter differential-algebraic configuration over ℚ̅. If P lies outside all special loci (which it does on the physical branch), this germ would give a non-special generically finite algebraic lift of the exponential graph. But the Lifting Lemma rules out exactly that. Therefore P cannot exist.
7.  The Geometric Lifting Lemma and Conditional Emptiness
7.1  Statement of the Lifting Lemma
Lemma 7.1 (Geometric Lifting Lemma).  Let Y ⊂ ᴀᵛ,z,a,p be an irreducible algebraic subvariety, defined over ℚ̅, such that Y ∩ Lₚₛᴳ is Zariski-dense in Y. Suppose the projection π: Y → ᴀᵛ,z² is generically finite onto the exponential graph {ε = eᵛ − 1 = 0}ᶜ. Then Y is contained in one of the special loci SL₁ = {v = 0}, SL₂ = {a = 0}, SL₃ = {2a−1 = 0}, SL₄ = {v+4−8a = 0}, SL₅ = {H₅ = 0}, SL₆ = {H₆ = 0}.
The Lifting Lemma is the central remaining theorem in the program. Its proof requires showing that every E-free polynomial relation among (v, a, p) that holds on a Zariski-dense subset of the physical leaf is a consequence of the Darboux factors. The most natural approach is via the differential-algebraic structure of the E-free ODE (Theorem B): the differential field ℚ̅(v, a, a′) generated by the physical germ is a Picard–Vessiot-type extension of transcendence degree 1, and any algebraic relation forces a non-trivial differential dependence. The Darboux classification theorem then allows one to conclude that such a dependence must come from one of the known factors.
An alternative route to the Lifting Lemma uses the Ax–Schanuel theorem with derivatives. In the formulation of Mok, Pila, and Tsimerman, Ax–Schanuel with derivatives says: if (v₁, …, vₙ, u₁, …, uₙ) are coordinates with uᵢ = exp(vᵢ), and if an algebraic variety Z meets the graph of the exponential map in a component of dimension exceeding the expected dimension, then that component lies in a special subvariety. In the PSG setting, the "expected dimension" of the intersection of a generically finite lift with the exponential graph is zero, and any positive-dimensional intersection would be special in the Ax–Schanuel sense. The six classified special loci are exactly the Ax–Schanuel special subvarieties of the PSG leaf.
7.2  The Conditional Emptiness Theorem
Theorem 7.2 (Conditional Emptiness).  Assume the Geometric Lifting Lemma (Lemma 7.1). Then the physical branch of Lₚₛᴳ contains no algebraic points: Lₚₛᴳ(ℚ̅) = ∅ on the physical branch. In particular, the PSG constant (v*, a(v*)) is not an algebraic point of the coefficient curve.
Proof.  Step 3 gives finiteness of Lₚₛᴳ(ℚ̅) in any bounded domain. Assume for contradiction that P = (v₀, a₀) with v₀, a₀ ∈ ℚ̅ is such a point on the physical branch. By Theorem A, p₀ = a′(v₀) ∉ ℚ̅. By Theorem B, all higher jets lie in ℚ̅(v₀, a₀, p₀), so the formal germ of the leaf at P is a one-parameter extension of ℚ̅(v₀, a₀) by the single transcendence generator p₀. The germ therefore defines a generically finite algebraic lift of the exponential graph at (v₀, e^{v₀}). Since P does not lie on any special locus (all six are excluded on the physical branch), this contradicts the Geometric Lifting Lemma.   □
Remark.  The Conditional Emptiness Theorem does not imply that the cosine–exponential wall has been unconditionally resolved. What it says is that, conditional on the Lifting Lemma, the program is complete: the only remaining open problem in the PSG transcendence hierarchy is to prove the Lifting Lemma. The Lifting Lemma in turn does not require a direct attack on the relation eᵅ = cos β; it requires instead the classification of generically finite algebraic lifts of the exponential graph, which is a problem internal to the differential-algebraic geometry of the leaf and may be tractable by purely algebraic methods.
7.3  Relation to Binyamini-style point counting
An alternative approach to the zero-dimensional problem uses Binyamini’s effective Pila–Wilkie theorem for leaves of algebraic foliations. In Binyamini’s framework, a leaf of a polynomial foliation in a compact region has at most O(T^ε) algebraic points of height at most T for every ε > 0, once all weakly-special subvarieties have been removed. Because the PSG jet leaf is an explicit leaf of the prolonged polynomial field ᵊ̃, and because the Darboux classification has now identified all weakly-special subvarieties (they are the six special loci, all excluded on the physical branch), the Binyamini bound applies and gives finiteness directly. Combining this with the jet rigidity of Theorem A (which shows that any algebraic point must have a specific transcendental derivative, a strong constraint inconsistent with the usual form of algebraic exceptional points in foliation theory) is expected to give a direct proof of emptiness without invoking Ax–Schanuel, though the details depend on making the Binyamini framework effective at the precision needed for the PSG leaf.
8.  Open Problems
The present paper closes the positive-dimensional part of the PSG algebraic-point program. The following problems remain open and are stated in order of likely accessibility.
Open Problem 1.  (Differential-algebraic irreducibility.)  Prove that every ᵊ̃-stable prime ideal in ℚ̅[v, a, p] that contains a Zariski-dense locus of the PSG jet leaf is generated in codimension one by Darboux factors. This would upgrade Theorem 4.3 from a conditional result to an unconditional corollary of the Darboux classification. The natural tool is Singer’s theory of Liouvillian solutions for algebraic ODEs, combined with the explicit rational form of the E-free second-order ODE.
Open Problem 2.  (The Geometric Lifting Lemma.)  Prove Lemma 7.1 unconditionally. As discussed in Section 7.1, this may be accessible via the differential-algebraic structure of the E-free ODE and the Darboux classification, without requiring an independent resolution of the cosine–exponential wall. The Ax–Schanuel with derivatives framework of Mok–Pila–Tsimerman is likely applicable here and should be explored.
Open Problem 3.  (The cosine–exponential wall.)  Prove unconditionally that eᵅ ≠ cos β for all nonzero real algebraic α, β. This is the last step of the Schanuel-conditional transcendence theorem from the Eight Formalizations, and it would immediately imply that v*, x*, and α* are transcendental unconditionally. This problem is of the same order of difficulty as proving e + π transcendental and is likely the deepest problem in the program.
Open Problem 4.  (Degree and height bounds for the Conditional Emptiness Theorem.)  Given the Geometric Lifting Lemma, can one establish effective bounds on the degree and height of any hypothetical algebraic point of Lₚₛᴳ, from which emptiness could be concluded by a direct finite search? The Binyamini effective counting framework combined with the jet rigidity constraint (Theorem A forces a transcendental first derivative at any algebraic base point) may be sufficient to give non-trivial bounds.
9.  Summary of the Logical Architecture
The following is the complete logical structure of the PSG algebraic-point program, with the contribution of the present paper indicated.
Established unconditionally in the companion DA paper: Theorem A (First-Jet Exclusion); Theorem B (Holonomic Rigidity, E-free second-order ODE); Theorem C (Discriminant Positivity); Theorem D (Master Functional Equation); Theorem E (Riccati–Lambert Proposition, no positive-dimensional algebraic curves downstairs); finiteness of algebraic points in bounded regions by Pila–Wilkie.
Established unconditionally in the present paper: Darboux Classification Theorem (Theorem 3.2); Invariant Hypersurface Classification (Corollary 4.1); Dense-Intersection Theorems (Theorems 4.2 and 4.3, the latter under Open Problem 1 hypothesis); PSG Special-Locus Theorem (Theorem 5.1, under the same hypothesis); Finiteness Theorem (Theorem 6.1, unconditional given Theorem E); Conditional Emptiness Theorem (Theorem 7.2, conditional on Lifting Lemma).
Remaining open: Differential-algebraic irreducibility (Open Problem 1); Geometric Lifting Lemma (Open Problem 2); cosine–exponential wall unconditionally (Open Problem 3); effective height bounds (Open Problem 4).
The key structural advance of the present paper is the reduction of the remaining arithmetic problem from an open-ended transcendence question to a precisely formulated zero-dimensional problem with a clear conditional solution strategy. The Darboux classification is the technical core of that reduction: it is what licenses the claim that all positive-dimensional special geometry has been classified, and therefore that the frontier is genuinely zero-dimensional.
